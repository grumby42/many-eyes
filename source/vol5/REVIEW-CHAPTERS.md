Chapter by chapter

### 1. The Giant in the Workshop: Florence, 1494–1504
**(a) Gaps.** Landucci carries too much weight alone. The chapter needs the other Florentine diarist-chroniclers who wrote day by day in the same decade:
- **Piero Parenti**, *Storia fiorentina*, a near-contemporaneous, anti-Savonarolan record (ed. A. Matucci, Olschki, 1994–2018). **Known.**
- **Bartolomeo Cerretani**, *Storia fiorentina* (ed. G. Berti, Olschki 1994). **Known.**
- **Tribaldo de' Rossi**, *Ricordanze* (in *Delizie degli eruditi toscani* vol. 23). **Known.**
- **Biagio Buonaccorsi**, *Diario* 1498–1512, written by Machiavelli's chancery colleague. **Known.**

The chapter is also missing the view from outside the city:
- **Marino Sanudo**'s *Diarii* (Venice, from 1496). It reports Florence through Venetian ambassadors. **Known.**
- **Johann Burchard**'s *Liber notarum* (papal master of ceremonies). It covers Savonarola's excommunication and death from Rome. **Known.**

The missing woman is **Isabella d'Este**, a patron and a woman's voice on the art. Her correspondence with Fra Pietro da Novellara describes Leonardo's studio on 3 and 14 April 1501 (Archivio di Stato di Mantova). **Known.**

**(b) Convergence days.**
- **7 April 1498**, the failed ordeal by fire: Landucci, Parenti, Cerretani, Sanudo's dispatches, and Savonarola's own later depositions.
- **23 May 1498**, the execution: Landucci, Parenti, Burchard, Sanudo.
- **14–18 May 1504**, the giant's journey: Landucci, Parenti, Opera payments.

**(c) Additions.** Add Parenti as a counter-diarist to Landucci, so the chapter gives positions on Savonarola from both camps. Add the Novellara letters, which give Leonardo a dated studio visit he otherwise lacks.

### 2. The Journal and the Poem: Alfoxden and Grasmere, 1797–1803
**(a) Gaps.** **Sara Hutchinson** is the person the "Dejection" letter was written to, yet she is not an eye. Her letters are in *The Letters of Sara Hutchinson* (ed. K. Coburn, 1954). **Known.** **Mary Hutchinson**, the bride of the wedding day, survives in *The Letters of Mary Wordsworth* (ed. M. E. Burton, 1958). The bulk is later, so the 1800–02 coverage is **check**. **Coleridge's Notebooks** (ed. Coburn, Bollingen) are dated almost daily in 1799–1803 and should be cited as their own series, apart from his letters. **Known.**

The political radical is **John Thelwall**, who visited Alfoxden in July 1797. That visit is what brought the spy Walsh down; Thelwall's letters to his wife survive. **Check** for the date and edition. The hostile reviewer missing from the chapter is **Francis Jeffrey**, whose first *Edinburgh Review* (October 1802) attacked the "Lake" sect through Southey's *Thalaba*. **Known.**

**(b) Convergence day.** **4 October 1802**: the wedding at Brompton, Dorothy's journal, "Dejection" in the *Morning Post*, and Coleridge's letters of the same week.

**(c) Additions.** Make Sara Hutchinson an eye. Add Jeffrey's October 1802 review so the "judged" strand covers more than Southey's 1798 review.

### 3. The Ninth: Vienna, 1822–1824
**(a) Gaps.** **Franz Schubert** wrote to Leopold Kupelwieser on 31 March 1824 about Beethoven's planned concert and his own symphonic ambitions (Deutsch, *Schubert: Die Dokumente seines Lebens*). **Known.** **Joseph Carl Rosenbaum**, a Viennese diarist who wrote daily from 1797 to 1829 (Haus-, Hof- und Staatsarchiv; selections ed. Radant, Haydn Yearbook 1968), is the best candidate for a non-musician's same-night entry. **Check** whether he attended on 7 May.

Several sides of the night are missing:
- Women in the household: **Nanette Streicher**'s letters and housekeeping notes with Beethoven (Beethoven-Haus Bonn). **Check** for 1824 items.
- Officials: the **censorship file** that forced the Missa solemnis movements to be billed as "hymns" (Austrian police and censorship records). **Check.**
- The theatre: impresario **Louis Antoine Duport**'s correspondence on the hall and fees. **Check.**
- The London institution: the **Philharmonic Society minute books** (British Library, RPS Archive). These are dated and already implied by the ledger. **Known.**

**(b) Convergence day.** **7 May 1824**: the conversation books, Kanne, the Wiener Zeitschrift, Schindler, Karl, and Rosenbaum if confirmed.

**(c) Additions.** Add Schubert's letter, which shows the next generation watching the premiere happen. Make the London minute books an eye in their own right, so the 1822 and 1825 ends of the chapter become a convergence of their own.

### 4. Impression: Paris, 1874
**(a) Gaps.** The chapter has six male critics but no woman critic. **Marc de Montifaud** (Marie-Amélie Chartroule de Montifaud) reviewed the show in *L'Artiste*, 1 May 1874. **Check** the exact issue.

Other reviewers of 1874 who could be used:
- **Jean Prouvaire**, *Le Rappel*, 20 April 1874. **Known.**
- **Émile Cardon**, *La Presse*, 29 April 1874. **Known.**
- **Armand Silvestre**, *L'Opinion nationale*, 22 April 1874. **Known.**

For the press abroad, **Henry James** reviewed the 1876 show for the *New York Tribune* (13 May 1876). **Known.** Morisot's mother **Cornélie Morisot**'s letters, in *Correspondance de Berthe Morisot* (ed. Rouart), give a domestic, sceptical voice. **Known.** **Pissarro**'s letters to **Théodore Duret** (Bailly-Herzberg, *Correspondance*, vol. 1) also belong here. **Known.**

**(b) Convergence days.**
- **25–29 April 1874**: Leroy, Castagnary, Prouvaire, Cardon, Silvestre, and the Goncourt journal.
- **24 March 1875**: Drouot, Burty's preface, Morisot letters, press.

**(c) Additions.** Add Montifaud. Add Henry James for 1876, which turns the April 1876 convergence transatlantic.

### 5. Vienna 1900: The Secession and the Diaries
**(a) Gaps.** **Theodor Herzl**'s diaries (1895–1904; *Briefe und Tagebücher*, Propyläen) are missing. He was feuilleton editor of the *Neue Freie Presse*, the paper the Secession fought in. **Known.**

Other dated voices to add:
- **Josef Redlich**'s political diary from 1908 (*Schicksalsjahre Österreichs*, ed. Fellner). It gives the official view. **Known.**
- **Bertha von Suttner**'s diaries (UN Library Geneva). **Known.**
- **Lou Andreas-Salomé**'s Freud journal, 1912–13 (*In der Schule bei Freud*). It gives a woman's dated view of Berggasse. **Known.**
- **Stefan Zweig**'s *Tagebücher* (from 1912) for 1913. **Known.**
- The workers' press: the *Arbeiter-Zeitung* reviews of the Secession and the Kunstschau. **Known.**

**(b) Convergence days.**
- **The 6th Secession exhibition, January 1900**, devoted to Japanese art from Adolf Fischer's collection, with reviews by Hevesi and others. This is a global hinge the chapter skips. **Known.**
- **31 March 1913**: Schnitzler, Schoenberg's and Berg's letters, Kraus, and the Viennese press.

**(c) Additions.** Add Herzl. Add the January 1900 Japanese show, which also threads to Impressionism and v2:black-ships.

### 6. The Rite of Spring: Paris, 29 May 1913
**(a) Gaps.** **Valentine Gross** (later Hugo) drew the dancers in performance on the nights themselves. Her drawings are dated visual records (Musée d'Orsay and Bibliothèque-musée de l'Opéra). **Known.**

Other gaps:
- **Puccini** saw the Rite in Paris in early June 1913 and wrote about it. **Check** the letter, its recipient and date.
- **Misia Sert**, Diaghilev's confidante, survives in letters. **Check.**
- The Russian view from home: reviews in St Petersburg papers such as *Rech'* and *Apollon*. **Check.**
- **Maurice Ravel**'s letters of June 1913 praising the work (Ravel, *Lettres, écrits*, ed. Orenstein). **Check** the dates.

**(b) Convergence day.** **2 June 1913**, the fourth performance: Van Vechten, Puccini if confirmed, Gross's drawings, and second-week press.

**(c) Additions.** Add Gross's drawings, the only same-night images of Nijinsky's lost choreography. Add one Russian-press voice from home.

### 7. The Bauhaus: Weimar, Dessau, Berlin, 1919–1933
**(a) Gaps.** The most important gap is global. In **December 1922** Bauhaus works (Klee, Kandinsky, Feininger and others) were shown in **Calcutta**, at the Indian Society of Oriental Art's exhibition, beside Gaganendranath and Abanindranath Tagore and others. Stella Kramrisch arranged it. The catalogue and the Calcutta press survive. **Known** (confirmed).

Japanese students are also missing:
- **Mizutani Takehiko**, at the Bauhaus 1927–29. **Known.**
- **Yamawaki Iwao and Michiko**, 1930–32. Iwao made the collage *Der Schlag gegen das Bauhaus* (1932). Michiko's memoir *Bauhaus to sadō* is late. **Known** for the people, **check** for the edition.

Women and the dead:
- **Otti Berger**, weaver, murdered at Auschwitz. Her letters are in the Bauhaus-Archiv. **Check.**
- **Friedl Dicker-Brandeis**, whose later Terezín teaching makes the thread to v1. **Known.**
- **Lucia Moholy**'s 1920s diaries and photographs. **Check.**
- **Tut Schlemmer**, the other half of Oskar's letters. **Known.**

**(b) Convergence days.**
- **11 April 1933**, the Steglitz search: Mies, Lilly Reich, student letters, Berlin press.
- **22 August 1932**, the Dessau council vote to close the school.

**(c) Additions.** Add the Calcutta 1922 show as a convergence. Add the Yamawakis, so the school is seen from outside Germany.

### 8. Harlem, 1921–1931
**(a) Gaps.** Music is nearly absent. **Duke Ellington**'s Cotton Club opening (4 December 1927) can be followed in the *New York Amsterdam News* and *Variety*. **Known.**

Other gaps:
- The Black press beyond the NAACP magazines: the *Pittsburgh Courier*, *Amsterdam News* and *Chicago Defender*. **Known.**
- Garveyism: **Marcus Garvey**'s *Negro World* (Hill, ed., *Marcus Garvey and UNIA Papers*). **Known.**
- Labour: **A. Philip Randolph** and the *Messenger*. **Known.**
- Women: **Nella Larsen**'s letters to Van Vechten (Beinecke). **Known.** **Gwendolyn Bennett**'s "Ebony Flute" column in *Opportunity*, 1926–28, and her 1925–26 diary (Schomburg Center). **Check** for the diary.
- The Atlantic: **René Maran**'s Goncourt for *Batouala* (December 1921) and **Paulette Nardal**'s *La Revue du monde noir* (Paris, 1931–32). **Known.**
- **Van Vechten**'s daybooks (*The Splendid Drunken Twenties*, ed. Kellner, 2003) are true dated diaries. Promote them above his letters. **Known.**

**(b) Convergence days.**
- **1 May 1925**: Opportunity dinner, the Van Vechten daybook, Dunbar-Nelson.
- **4 December 1927**: the Ellington opening and Hurston's Mason contract.

**(c) Additions.** Add the Van Vechten daybooks and the *Negro World*. Add Paulette Nardal for the 1931 close, which ties Harlem to Paris négritude.
