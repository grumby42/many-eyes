## Written in the Stars: Babylon Watches Alexander (331–323 BC) [id babylon-331]
Intro: Every night for some six hundred years, scholars attached to Esagila, the great temple of Marduk in Babylon, went up to watch the sky and wrote down what they saw: where the moon stood against the stars, which planets had risen, whether clouds hid everything. At the end of each entry, and at the end of each month, they added the things that happened on the ground — the price of barley and dates, the height of the Euphrates, fires, plagues, the movements of kings. Modern scholars call these tablets the Astronomical Diaries. They were not personal diaries; no single scribe signs them, and the observations were later copied and compiled. But they are dated to the night, written within days or weeks of the events, and kept by people who had no stake in flattering a Macedonian king.
