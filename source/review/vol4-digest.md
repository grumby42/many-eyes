## A Black Drop on the Sun: The Transits of Venus (1760–1771) [id transits-of-venus]
Intro: On 6 June 1761 and again on 3–4 June 1769, Venus passed across the face of the sun, a pair of events that would not come again until 1874 and 1882. Edmond Halley had shown in 1716 that if observers far apart on the Earth timed the planet's entry and exit, the small differences in their times would give the distance to the sun, the unit by which the whole solar system is measured. To do it, astronomers had to be at the ends of the Earth at the same hour, with good clocks, known positions and clear skies. What followed was the first planned world-wide scientific collaboration. In 1761, despite the Seven Years' War, about 120 observers at some 60 sites took part; in 1769, about 150 observations came from 77 stations, from Hudson Bay to Tahiti and from Vardø to Baja California.
