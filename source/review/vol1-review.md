# Review: Many Eyes, Volume I (wars, revolutions, disasters)

**known** = confident the dated record exists and is published/archived; **check** = verify first.

## Verdict

The volume's strength is its convergences: Magdeburg, the London Fire, Petrograd in February 1917 and Ginling's gate are laid out across genuinely opposed sides, and Nanjing includes Japanese soldiers' own records. The Russia 1917 and WW2 Europe chapters are the densest and best balanced. The volume is thinnest outside Europe and its settler offshoots. Apart from Nanjing, Japan and a few Saint-Domingue colonists, there is no African, Ottoman, South Asian or Latin American vantage point, and the colonised appear mainly as people others wrote about. Within the chapters there are big chronological holes: no 1862 or 1864 in the Civil War, one 1917 entry in WW1, and no Thermidor in the French Revolution. Finally, "Heian" (954–1021) is a court chapter rather than a war chapter and sits outside the volume's span. It belongs in Volume II or VI.

## Chapter by chapter

### The Moon Without a Flaw (heian)
- **Gaps:** It hears from no one outside the court and no foreigner. Fujiwara no Takaie's Dazaifu dispatches on the 1019 Toi (Jurchen) raids on Tsushima, Iki and northern Kyushu survive in *Shōyūki* and *Chōya gunsai* (known), and Goryeo's return of captives in 1019 is recorded on the Korean side in *Goryeosa* (known). Akazome Emon's poetry collection is a third salon voice (known).
- **Convergences:** 1019 (4th–6th month): the Toi raids, Sanesuke's anxious entries, Michinaga's taking of the tonsure, and the Sarashina girl's eastern provinces.
- **Additions:** Add the 1019 raid as the chapter's one "disaster" day. Otherwise, move the chapter to Volume II.

### Ash on the Wind (thirty-years)
- **Gaps:** The princely and diplomatic layer is missing, and so are the Swedish, Spanish and Bohemian-Protestant views.
  - Christian II of Anhalt-Bernburg's enormous diary (digital edition, Herzog August Bibliothek Wolfenbüttel; known). He fought and was captured at the White Mountain.
  - Pavel Skála ze Zhoře, *Historie česká* (exile chronicle; known).
  - Axel Oxenstierna's correspondence (*Rikskansleren Axel Oxenstiernas skrifter och brefvexling*; known).
  - Prioress Klara Staiger of Mariastein near Eichstätt, a nun's diary from 1631 onward (known; studied by Woodford, *Nuns as Historians*).
  - Jürgen Ackermann, a captain in the Pappenheim regiment (check).
  - At the peace: the Acta Pacis Westphalicae edition of the diaries of nuncio Fabio Chigi and imperial envoy Isaak Volmar (known).
- **Convergences:**
  - 8 Nov 1620 (White Mountain): Anhalt, Slavata and the Prague chroniclers.
  - 6/16 Nov 1632 (Lützen and Gustavus's death): Monro, Hagendorf, Heberle and Oxenstierna.
  - 25 Feb 1634 (Wallenstein murdered at Eger).
- **Additions:** Add a White Mountain cluster built on Anhalt's diary. Expand the 1648 peace day with Chigi and Volmar.

### The Bills, the Bonfire and the Fire (london-1666)
- **Gaps:** There are no women, no workers and no scientific correspondents.
  - The Verney family letters (Verney Memoirs; Claydon House). They include Lady Hobart's Fire letter of September 1666 (check the date).
  - Thomas Rugge's *Diurnall* (BL Add MS 10116–7; known).
  - Henry Oldenburg's letters to Boyle (*Correspondence*, ed. Hall & Hall; known).
  - The *London Gazette* no. 85 (3–10 Sept 1666; known), a government print record.
  - George Fox and Quaker "sufferings" records (check for dated 1665 entries).
- **Convergences:**
  - The week of 19 Sept 1665 (peak bill), with Oldenburg, Rugge and Josselin added.
  - 5 Sept 1666, the day the fire was checked, when Pepys, Evelyn and Taswell can be joined by Rugge and the Gazette.
- **Additions:** Add Oldenburg as the bridge to the Royal Society, and the Verney letters for women's and country-house reception.

### Rien (french-rev)
- **Gaps:** The chapter needs sans-culottes, the provinces, the counter-revolution, the enslaved and the press abroad.
  - Lucile Desmoulins's fragmentary journal, 1788–93 (known).
  - The Marquis de Ferrières's letters to his wife from the Assembly (*Correspondance inédite*, 1932; known).
  - Axel von Fersen's diary for Varennes, 20–21 June 1791 (ed. Klinckowström; known).
  - Dr John Moore, *A Journal during a Residence in France* (Aug–Dec 1792, including the September massacres; known).
  - British embassy despatches (Browning ed., *Despatches from Paris*; known).
  - On Saint-Domingue, the 1791–92 letters of the insurgent leaders Jean-François and Biassou to the civil commissioners (known; printed in Dubois & Garrigus, *Slave Revolution in the Caribbean*).
  - Ebubekir Ratib Efendi's *sefâretnâme* from Vienna, 1791–92 (known), an Ottoman reading of revolutionary Europe.
- **Convergences:**
  - 20–21 June 1791: Fersen, Madame Royale, Morris and Hardy.
  - 2–6 Sept 1792: Moore, Ruault, Restif and Jullien.
  - 9–10 Thermidor (27–28 July 1794): currently absent.
- **Additions:** Add a Thermidor cluster. Give the insurgents' own letters next to the planters', so that Saint-Domingue is not seen only through white eyes.

### The Road to Moscow and Back (russia-1812)
- **Gaps:** There are almost no Russian women, peasants, Jews or Baltic voices.
  - Maria Volkova's 1812 letters to Varvara Lanskaya (published as *Griboedovskaia Moskva v pis'makh M. A. Volkovoi*; known).
  - Kutuzov's letters to his wife (known).
  - Boris Uxkull's dated diary as a Russian cavalry officer, published in English as *Arms and the Woman* (known).
  - Boniface de Castellane's *Journal*, a daily journal at imperial headquarters (known).
  - Shneur Zalman of Liadi's letter urging support for the Tsar (known; date check). It gives a Jewish Pale view.
  - Label retrospective memoirs (Bourgogne, Walter) as such.
- **Convergences:**
  - 18–19 Aug (Valutino) and 7 Sept N.S. (Borodino): Uxkull, Chicherin and Castellane.
  - 19 Oct, leaving Moscow: Fusil, Pokhorskaya and Ségur.
- **Additions:** Add Volkova for the view from Moscow's evacuated gentry. Add Uxkull to double the Russian front-line eyes.

### A House Divided (us-civil-war)
- **Gaps:** It has no Black soldier's own dated letters, no Western or Native vantage point and no foreign working-class one, and 1862 and 1864 are almost empty.
  - Cpl. James Henry Gooding's letters to the *New Bedford Mercury*, 54th Massachusetts, 1863 (known).
  - The Freedmen and Southern Society Project, *Freedom* series (known).
  - Edmund Ruffin's diary at Sumter (LSU edition; known).
  - Sarah Morgan's Baton Rouge diary (known).
  - Emma Balfour's diary of the siege of Vicksburg (known).
  - Maria Lydig Daly's New York diary (known).
  - John Hay's diary (known).
  - Benjamin Moran's journal at the US legation in London (known).
  - The Manchester working men's address to Lincoln of 31 Dec 1862 and his reply of 19 Jan 1863 (known).
- **Convergences:**
  - 17 Sept 1862 (Antietam).
  - 13–16 July 1863, the New York draft riots: Strong, Daly, Welles and Gooding (writing about Fort Wagner on 18 July).
  - 9 April 1865 (Appomattox).
- **Additions:** Add a draft-riots and Fort Wagner cluster. Put the Lancashire address beside the 1 January 1863 Camp Saxton day.

### Swimming in the Afternoon (ww1)
- **Gaps:**
  - 1916–17 is nearly empty: there is no Verdun, no Caporetto, no Easter Rising and no Armenian genocide.
  - Colonial soldiers' voices are thin. The censored letters of Indian soldiers survive in BL IOR/L/MIL/5/825, ed. Omissi, *Indian Voices of the Great War* (known).
  - Henry Morgenthau's diary (ed. Sarafian; known) and Consul Leslie Davis's Harput reports (US State Department records, quotable; known).
  - Michel Corday, *L'envers de la guerre* (known).
  - Carlo Emilio Gadda's *Giornale di guerra e di prigionia*, covering Caporetto (known).
  - James Stephens, *The Insurrection in Dublin*, dated daily in 1916 (known).
- **Convergences:**
  - 24–29 April 1916 (the Rising, Kut and the Verdun spring).
  - 24–27 Oct 1917 (Caporetto against the Bolshevik eve).
- **Additions:** Add a 1915 Armenia cluster with Morgenthau, Davis and Turjman (already in the chapter). Add a 1917 cluster with Gadda.

### Two Calendars, One Collapse (russia-1917)
- **Gaps:** Soldiers, workers, the non-Russian peripheries and monarchists are under-weighted.
  - *Soldatskie pis'ma 1917 goda* (1927; known).
  - Yuri Got'e's Moscow diary (*Time of Troubles*, Princeton 1988; known).
  - Alexandre Benois's *Dnevnik 1916–1918* (known).
  - Lev Tikhomirov's diary (check the years).
  - Volodymyr Vynnychenko's diary for Kyiv and the Rada (known).
  - Ambassador David R. Francis's letters, plus FRUS 1918 Russia (US government records; known).
  - Sukhanov is a memoir and should be labelled as such.
- **Convergences:**
  - 3–5 July (July Days), adding Benois and Got'e.
  - 5–6 Jan 1918, the Constituent Assembly, adding Francis and Vynnychenko.
- **Additions:** Add a Kyiv (Rada) strand so the collapse covers the empire, not two capitals.

### The Gate at Ginling (nanjing-1937)
- **Gaps:** There are few Chinese voices beyond Cheng Ruifang, and nothing from Washington or the Panay.
  - Jiang Gonggu, *Xianjing sanyue ji* (Three Months in the Fallen Capital, 1938; known).
  - Guo Qi, *Xiandu xuelei lu* (check).
  - On the Japanese side: the diaries of Iinuma Mamoru (SEF chief of staff) and Uemura Toshimichi, both in Kaikōsha, *Nankin senshi shiryōshū* (known). Also Foreign Ministry official Ishii Itarō's diary (known).
  - FRUS 1937 vol. IV on the USS Panay sinking of 12 Dec, and US consul James Espy's January 1938 report (known).
  - The dispatches of Tillman Durdin (NYT) and Archibald Steele (Chicago Daily News) (known).
- **Convergences:** 12–13 Dec, the Panay and the fall of the city, which would add US Navy and State Department records to the existing day.
- **Additions:** Add Jiang Gonggu and the Panay day.

### Only Tanya Is Left (ww2-europe)
- **Gaps:** There are no non-Jewish Polish, Italian, Ukrainian or Greek civilians, and no German military-elite voices.
  - Zygmunt Klukowski's Szczebrzeszyn diary (known).
  - Iris Origo, *War in Val d'Orcia*, 1943–44 (known).
  - Iryna Khoroshunova's Kyiv diary, which covers Babi Yar (known).
  - Ulrich von Hassell's diaries (known).
  - The Halder war diary (known).
  - Warning: the *Letzte Briefe aus Stalingrad* are of disputed authenticity and should be avoided.
- **Convergences:**
  - 7 Sept 1940 (the Blitz begins): Nicolson, Last, Kellner, Klemperer and Lindgren.
  - 29–30 Sept 1941 (Babi Yar).
  - 31 Jan–2 Feb 1943 (Stalingrad's surrender): Grossman, Goebbels, Kellner and Klemperer.
- **Additions:** Add a Stalingrad cluster and the Warsaw Uprising of 1 Aug 1944, using Klukowski and Ringelblum's archive as context.

### Under the Scorching Sun (japan-1945)
- **Gaps:** It is missing Okinawa, Manchuria, Korean victims, the Japanese decision-makers, Allied POWs and China.
  - Kido Kōichi's diary (known).
  - Admiral Ugaki Matome's *Sensōroku*, translated as *Fading Victory*, which ends on 15 Aug 1945 (known).
  - *Kike wadatsumi no koe*, student soldiers' letters (known).
  - Fr. John Siemes's Hiroshima account (known).
  - Interim Committee minutes of 31 May 1945 (US government record; known).
  - Chiang Kai-shek's diaries (Hoover Institution; known).
  - Weary Dunlop's POW diaries (known).
- **Convergences:**
  - 9 Aug, the Soviet invasion of Manchuria the same day as Nagasaki: Kido, Ugaki and Truman.
  - 14–15 Aug: Kido, Ugaki's last entry, Chiang and the existing broadcast diarists.
- **Additions:** Add Kido and Ugaki to give the "halt" a decision-room view. Add Chiang for 15 August.

### The Long Threads (long-threads)
- **Gaps:** It jumps from 1666 to 1832.
  - Philadelphia yellow fever, 1793: Elizabeth Drinker's diary (known), Benjamin Rush's letters to his wife (known), and Absalom Jones and Richard Allen's *Narrative* (1794; known), a Black nurses' rebuttal.
  - Marseille plague, 1720: Bishop Belsunce's pastoral letters (check).
- **Convergence:** Oct 1793: Drinker, Rush and Jones–Allen on the same weeks; Saint-Domingue refugees link it to french-rev.

## Threads

1. **Saint-Domingue (french-rev) and Camp Saxton (us-civil-war).** Higginson and Charlotte Forten both wrote on Toussaint, so the two chapters are linked by emancipation at arms.
2. **Guericke at Magdeburg (thirty-years) and Vol IV *The Notebook*.** The man who survived the 1631 sack ran the Magdeburg hemispheres experiment in 1654.
3. **The 1665–66 plague (london-1666) and Vol IV.** Newton's Woolsthorpe notebooks were kept while the Bills of Mortality rose.
4. **The Westphalian congress (thirty-years) and Potsdam (japan-1945), via Vol VI *The Room*.** Two peace conferences, each recorded in delegates' diaries.
5. **The imperial train at Pskov (russia-1917), Napoleon's sleigh (russia-1812), the Enola Gay (japan-1945) and Lincoln's funeral train, via Vol VII *Getting There*.**
6. **Whitman (us-civil-war), Kollwitz (ww1) and Faber du Faur (russia-1812), via Vol V *The Studio*.** The war artist's dated sketchbook.
7. **Rabe (nanjing-1937) and Berlin (ww2-europe).** Rabe's January 1938 report to Hitler went unanswered, and Rosen's dispatches were filed in the Wilhelmstrasse.

## Beyond this volume

- **"The Colonised Eye" (resistance and empire, 1600–1945):** Haiti 1791–1804; Delhi 1857; the Herero war of 1904 (missionary diaries and German Reichstag records); Amritsar 1919 (Hunter Committee evidence); Ethiopia 1935–36.
- **"Famine Ledgers" (disaster as slow time):** Ireland 1845–49; Madras 1876–78; Russia 1891–92 (Tolstoy's relief letters); the Holodomor of 1932–33 (consular reports); Bengal 1943.
- **"The Long Siege" (single-place, day-by-day format):** Vienna 1683; Paris 1870–71 (Goncourt journal, balloon-post letters); Kut 1916; Madrid 1936–39; Leningrad 1941–44.
