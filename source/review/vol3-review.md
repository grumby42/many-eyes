# Review — Many Eyes, Volume III · Edges of the Map

Reviewer: historian of exploration and cross-cultural encounter. Labels: **known** = I am confident the source exists and is dated; **check** = plausible, verify before use.

## Verdict

The volume is at its best where the record is plural by nature: the Corps of Discovery (seven journal-keepers on one page), the Narrow Road (Bashō against Sora's notebook), Livingstone's spectrally imaged field diary against the *Last Journals*, and the North Pole's two telegrams with the Inughuit testimony. It deals honestly with sources of different kinds, is right to treat oral tradition and winter counts as dated records, and is consistently fair on contested questions (Cook against Peary, Ujiji's date, the Chitambo death date). It is thinnest in three places. Lower-deck and support-staff voices are often missing even where their journals are in print (Endeavour, South Pole, Everest). The "hosts" in the Asian chapters are under-used: the volume leans on Ming and Timurid court writing and gives little from Vietnam, Ryukyu, Byzantium or Armenia. And across the whole book the traffic runs mostly one way, with Europeans arriving. No chapter yet shows non-Western travellers looking at Europe or America, which is the clearest gap in the volume's theme.

## Chapter by chapter

### The Treasure Fleets (1405–1436)
- **Gaps.** The Red Sea and Indian Ocean hosts are thin outside Yemen. Add al-Maqrizi, *Kitab al-Suluk*, which has year-by-year entries on Chinese junks at Aden and Jeddah in 835 AH/1432 (check). Add Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, *Inba' al-ghumr*, a dated annual chronicle (known; whether it mentions the junks is a check). For Đại Việt under Ming occupation from 1407, the *Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư* (known) is the same Yongle expansionism seen from the receiving end. The Ryukyuan *Rekidai Hōan*, diplomatic correspondence from 1424 (known; Okinawa Prefectural Archives edition), shows the tribute system the fleets enforced. For a cartographic eye, the Mao Kun map in *Wubei zhi* (1621, known) is the fleet's route as it was remembered.
- **Convergence.** 1432–33 in the Red Sea: Ma Huan's Mecca party, the Rasulid chronicle, Ibn Taghribirdi and al-Maqrizi can all go on one page.
- **Add.** Al-Maqrizi alongside Ibn Taghribirdi, so the Mamluk view has two voices. A short Đại Việt sidebar on what the same emperor's armies were doing in 1407.

### Embassies to Samarkand and Beijing (1402–1422)
- **Gaps.** Armenian: Tovma Metsopetsi, *History of Timur-Lang and his Successors* (known; tr. Bedrosian), plus Armenian manuscript colophons (known; Sanjian, *Colophons of Armenian Manuscripts 1301–1480*). Byzantine: the letters of Manuel II Palaiologos (known; Dennis ed., Dumbarton Oaks 1977). Latin: John, archbishop of Sultaniyeh, *Libellus de notitia orbis* (1404, known), and Timur's 1402 letter to Charles VI (known; Archives nationales). Timurid: Hafiz-i Abru, who preserved Ghiyath al-Din's account (known). Korean: the *Joseon Wangjo Sillok* for Ming court events of 1420–21 (check; the Sillok are known, the specific entries are not).
- **Convergence.** 28 July 1402, Ankara: Schiltberger, Nizam al-Din Shami, Ibn Arabshah, Tovma Metsopetsi and Manuel II (who was in the West and heard the news there).
- **Add.** Tovma Metsopetsi, the victims' view that is currently missing. Manuel II's letters, which give the Byzantine relief at Ankara.

### The Narrow Road (1689)
- **Gaps.** Bashō's own dated letters from 1689 (known; Kon Eizō ed., *Bashō shokan taikan*). The VOC Dejima *Dagregisters* for 1689–92 (known; Nationaal Archief, NFJ series; Leiden *Deshima Dagregisters* edition) give a second foreign eye beyond Kaempfer. Kaibara Ekiken's travel diaries (known as a corpus; whether one falls in 1689–92 is a check) show a Confucian scholar walking the same roads. The chapter is about the encounter between metropolitan and provincial culture, so domain or checkpoint (sekisho) records for the Shirakawa or Ichiburi crossings would help (check, local archives). Women are absent, and the Ichiburi courtesans are very probably literary: say so.
- **Convergence.** Spring 1691: Kaempfer's court journey on the Tōkaidō while Bashō is in Kyoto and Ōmi (both known; the exact overlap is a check).
- **Add.** A Dagregister entry for a 1689 day of the journey, so Dejima's clerks and Bashō share a date.

### The Transit and the Shore (1768–1771)
- **Gaps.** Lower-deck and officer journals in print: Richard Pickersgill (known), Robert Molyneux (known, master's log), Zachary Hicks and John Gore logs (known; Beaglehole prints extracts), and James Magra's anonymous *Journal of a Voyage Round the World* (1771, known). Batavia: the VOC *Dagregister* of Batavia Castle (known; Arsip Nasional Republik Indonesia). Tahiti: Tupaia's own drawings (known; British Library Add MS 15508) are a dated visual record, not only something seen through him.
- **Convergence.** 3 June 1769 is the best day in the volume that is not being used. Laid beside Point Venus: William Wales at Prince of Wales Fort, Hudson Bay (known; journal in *Phil. Trans.*); Maximilian Hell at Vardø (known); Jeremiah Dixon at Hammerfest (known); Chappe d'Auteroche at San José del Cabo, Baja California, where he died of typhus (known; *Voyage en Californie*, 1772); David Rittenhouse at Norriton, Pennsylvania (known).
- **Add.** A global transit page with those five eyes. Magra as the sceptical voice from the crew.

### Humboldt's America (1799–1804)
- **Gaps.** Carlos Montúfar's diary, Quito to Mexico, including Chimborazo (known; compared with Humboldt's in *HiN* 2011, Potsdam). Cuba: Francisco de Arango y Parreño (known; dated memorials) for the planter society Humboldt criticised. Mexico: the *Gazeta de México* (known) and Andrés Manuel del Río (check). The enslaved people of the Cumaná and Havana markets appear only through Humboldt. Spanish notarial or slave-sale registers for Cumaná in 1799–1800 would be a real record of them (check, Archivo General de Indias).
- **Convergence.** 23 June 1802, Chimborazo: Humboldt, Bonpland and Montúfar on one page.
- **Add.** Montúfar for the Chimborazo convergence. A *Gazeta de México* notice of the travellers' arrival.

### Ocian in View: The Corps of Discovery (1803–1806)
- **Gaps.** Pierre-Antoine Tabeau's narrative from among the Arikara, 1803–05 (known; Abel ed., 1939). Charles McKenzie's Mandan narratives (known; Masson, *Les Bourgeois*). Alexander Henry the Younger's July 1806 visit to the Mandan and Hidatsa (known; Gough ed., Champlain Society). More winter counts: Battiste Good, American Horse (known; Mallery 1886/1893). York is the outstanding absence. He left no writing, but Clark's letters to his brother Jonathan (known; Holmberg, *Dear Brother*, 2002) carry his post-expedition story. Spanish attempts to intercept: Pedro Vial's and Facundo Melgares's parties (check).
- **Convergence.** 8–12 October 1804 at the Arikara villages: Lewis, Clark, Ordway, Gass, Whitehouse and Tabeau.
- **Add.** Tabeau for that October convergence. A York sidebar built from Clark's letters, threaded to Henson.

### Where the Nile Begins (1857–1864)
- **Gaps.** John and Katherine Petherick, the Khartoum consul who missed Speke at Gondokoro (known; *Travels in Central Africa*, 1869, with Katherine's narrative), give the other side of a bitter dispute. Alexine Tinne, the Dutch traveller on the White Nile in 1862–63, wrote letters that survive (known as a traveller; that she was at Gondokoro in February 1863 is a check). Johannes Rebmann's CMS journals (known; Cadbury Research Library, Birmingham) record the "snow mountains" and lakes debate from Rabai. On the Ganda side, Apolo Kaggwa's *Basekabaka be Buganda* (1901, known) is a later chronicle but gives Muteesa's court its own voice. Livingstone was at Bath in September 1864 (known; his journal).
- **Convergence.** February 1863 at Gondokoro: Speke, Grant, Samuel Baker, Petherick, and possibly Tinne.
- **Add.** The Pethericks. Livingstone's diary entry on Speke's death, which makes the hinge into the next chapter.

### Ujiji, 1871: Two Diaries, One Voice (1866–1874)
- **Gaps.** Stanley's actual field diaries, set against *How I Found Livingstone* (known; Stanley Archives, Royal Museum for Central Africa, Tervuren; Bennett ed. 1970). Verney Lovett Cameron, who met the body party at Unyanyembe in October 1873 (known; *Across Africa*, 1877). Bartle Frere's 1873 Zanzibar mission and the slave-trade treaty of 5 June 1873 (known; FO papers, *Parliamentary Papers*). Kalulu, Stanley's boy servant, appears only through Stanley.
- **Convergence.** October 1873 at Unyanyembe: Cameron, Jacob Wainwright, Chuma and Susi.
- **Add.** Cameron at Unyanyembe. Frere's treaty, a month after Chitambo, closing the loop on Nyangwe.

### Fram (1893–1896)
- **Gaps.** Eva Nansen's letters (known; National Library of Norway, Nansen papers). Eduard von Toll, who laid depots on the New Siberian Islands for Nansen (known). The Nenets and Russian traders at Khabarova, Yugor Strait, in August 1893 (through Nansen, known; Russian-side records are a check). Albert Armitage and Reginald Koettlitz of Jackson's party (known; Armitage, *Cadet to Commodore*). Norwegian press: *Verdens Gang*, *Aftenposten* (known).
- **Convergence.** August 1896: Fram comes out at Svalbard while Andrée's balloon party waits at Danskøya/Virgohamna. Whether the ship called there is a check (*Farthest North* vol. 2). Andrée's 1896 records are known.
- **Add.** Andrée at Virgohamna (it also threads to Vol VII). Eva Nansen, the home front the chapter lacks.

### Two Telegrams from the Top of the World (1908–1909)
- **Gaps.** Dr John W. Goodsell, the *Roosevelt*'s surgeon (known; *On Polar Trails*, 1983, from his manuscript). Minik Wallace, the Inughuit youth brought to New York in 1897, who made newspaper statements in 1909 (known). Josephine Peary's letters (check; Peary papers, US National Archives). The Danish colonial administration at Upernavik and Copenhagen, including Jens Daugaard-Jensen (check). The *New York Times* (Peary) set against the *New York Herald* (Cook), day by day in September 1909 (known).
- **Convergence.** 4–7 September 1909, Copenhagen, Cook's reception: Gibbs, *Politiken*, the University and Rasmussen, beside Peary's Indian Harbour cable.
- **Add.** Minik, a Greenlandic eye inside the American press frenzy. Goodsell, the doubting doctor.

### Five Flags on the Ice (1910–1913)
- **Gaps.** Hjalmar Johansen at Framheim (known; diaries, and the Framheim quarrel of 1911, in Roland Huntford). William Lashly's diary (known; *Under Scott's Command*, 1969). Oates's letters to his mother (known). Xavier Mertz's diary (known; in German, ed. Mertz family/Mawson Centre) and B. E. S. Ninnis's diary (known, Mitchell Library). The Japanese account *Nankyoku-ki* (1913, known). Felix König of Filchner's party (check). The *Mercury* of Hobart, 7–8 March 1912 (known).
- **Convergence.** 14 December 1912: Ninnis falls into a crevasse. Mertz's and Mawson's diaries cover the same day, while the Terra Nova party prepares for the relief voyage.
- **Add.** Johansen, which completes the Fram thread. Mertz's diary against Mawson's for December 1912–January 1913.

### Everest, 1953
- **Gaps.** Sherpas beyond Tenzing: Ang Nyima, Da Namgyal, Annullu. Oral history exists (check; Sherpa testimony in later collections such as Ortner's *Life and Death on Mt. Everest*). Ralph Izzard of the *Daily Mail*, the rival reporter (known; *The Innocent on Everest*, 1954). The Nepali press: *Gorkhapatra* (known) on the nationality row. The Tengboche lama (check). Mass Observation coronation-day diaries, 2 June 1953 (known; Mass Observation Archive, University of Sussex), would show the news arriving.
- **Convergence.** 2 June 1953: Morris's coded cable, The Times, Mass Observation diarists and the Calcutta press.
- **Add.** Mass Observation, a crowd of real dated eyes for the "news chain". Izzard as the outsider who was not under the Times contract.

## New chapters for this volume

1. **The First Fleet at Warrane / Sydney Cove (1787–1792).** Many officers and convicts kept diaries, a French expedition was in Botany Bay the same week, and the earliest letter by an Aboriginal writer survives. Eyes: Ralph Clark, diary (known); Arthur Bowes Smyth, journal (known); William Bradley, journal (known); Watkin Tench, narratives (known); Philip Gidley King, journal (known); La Pérouse, journal and letters from Botany Bay, Jan–Mar 1788 (known); Bennelong, letter of 1796 (known).
2. **The Macartney Embassy (1792–1794).** British and Qing records cover the same audience day by day, and a child kept a diary. Eyes: Lord Macartney, journal (known); George Thomas Staunton, aged 12, diary (known, British Library); Aeneas Anderson, narrative (known); William Alexander, journal and drawings (known); John Barrow (known); Qianlong's edicts and court memorials (known; First Historical Archives, published 1996).
3. **The Second Kamchatka Expedition (1733–1743).** Many scientists and officers kept records, and the Aleut encounter of 1741 is seen by several of them. Eyes: Georg Wilhelm Steller, journal (known); Sven Waxell, account (known); Stepan Krasheninnikov (known); Johann Georg Gmelin, travels (known); Gerhard Friedrich Müller (known); Aleksei Chirikov, reports (known).
4. **Searching for Franklin (1845–1859).** Navy journals, Inuit testimony recorded on dated days, and a wife who ran her own campaign. Eyes: John Rae, 1854 report and journals (known); Francis McClintock, *Voyage of the Fox* (known); Charles Francis Hall, Inuit testimony notebooks (known, Smithsonian); Jane Franklin, journals (known, Scott Polar Research Institute); Sophia Cracroft, letters (known); Inuit witnesses such as In-nook-poo-zhee-jook (through Rae and Hall, known).
5. **The Iwakura Mission (1871–1873).** The encounter runs the other way: Japan observes America and Europe, and the hosts' press and governments wrote back. Eyes: Kume Kunitake, *Beiō Kairan Jikki* (known); Kido Takayoshi, diary (known); Ōkubo Toshimichi, letters (known); Queen Victoria, journal (known); Ulysses Grant administration and US press (known).
6. **Burke and Wills (1860–1861).** Field notebooks, the Depot party, an Aboriginal rescue and a Royal Commission. Eyes: William Wills, journal (known); Robert O'Hara Burke, notebook (known, State Library Victoria); William Brahe, testimony (known); John King, narrative (known); Hermann Beckler, diary (known); Royal Commission minutes, 1862 (known); Yandruwandha people (through King and later oral history, check).

## Threads

1. **The guides written out of the story:** Tupaia (Endeavour) → Sacagawea (Corps) → Sidi Mubarak Bombay (Nile) → Chuma and Susi (Livingstone) → Uutaaq (North Pole) → Tenzing (Everest).
2. **York → Matthew Henson** (Corps → North Pole): Black members of famous expeditions and how the record dealt with them.
3. **Hjalmar Johansen** (Fram → South Pole): the same man at 86°14′ and humiliated at Framheim.
4. **The transit of Venus, 1769 (Endeavour) → the transit of Mercury, 1802 (Humboldt) → Vol IV, The Notebook**: the measurement of the solar system.
5. **Andrée at Virgohamna (Fram) → Vol VII, Getting There**: the balloon as polar transport. The Norge airship of 1926 (Amundsen, Nobile, Ellsworth) could link both.
6. **Bath 1864 (Nile) → Vol VI, The Room**: the BA meeting as a "meeting of minds" that did not happen. Copenhagen 1909 (North Pole) also fits.
7. **Clavijo and Chen Cheng (Embassies) → Iwakura (proposed)**: embassies as encounters, centuries apart.
8. **Kaempfer at Dejima (Narrow Road) → v2:black-ships**: the Dutch window before Perry.

## Beyond this volume

- **The Return Gaze**: non-Western travellers in Europe and America. Example chapters: Yirmisekiz Mehmed Çelebi in Paris (1720–21); Mirza Abu Taleb Khan in London (1799–1802); the Iwakura Mission (1872–73); Naser al-Din Shah at the Vienna Exposition (1873); Guo Songtao in London (1877–79).
- **Pilgrim Roads**: journeys made for faith, with the travellers' records set beside those of the hosts and the shrines. Example chapters: Ibn Jubayr's hajj (1183–85); Margery Kempe to Jerusalem (1413–15); the Shikoku pilgrimage diaries (Edo period); Santiago in the 1490s (Hieronymus Münzer); the 1853 hajj (Burton set beside Arabic and Ottoman sources).
- **The Logbook**: a format built only from ships' logs and weather registers, where the "eyes" are watch entries on the same ocean days. Example chapters: the Atlantic in the winter of 1782–83; the East India fleets in the monsoon of 1810; the whaling grounds in 1846; HMS Challenger (1872–76); the Old Weather WWI convoys.
