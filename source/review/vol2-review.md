# Many Eyes, Volume II: Specialist Review

Sources are named from my own knowledge, with no web searches. **known** means I am confident the source exists, is dated and is published or archived. **check** means it is plausible and needs verifying before use.

## 1. Verdict

Volume II is at its best where the record really is many-sided. Imjin, Vienna, the Black Ships and Moscow 1937 set enemy and outsider diaries side by side and are honest about what kind of record each one is. The opening chapters (Merer, Babylon, Vesuvius) handle the absence of diaries well by treating administrative records as the "eyes", and this is the volume's most original move. It is thinnest in four places. **Official chronicles of the state are missing:** the Joseon Sillok, the Ming and Qing Shilu and the Mutiny Papers are absent even though they are dated day by day. **Local voices are thin in the colonial disasters:** Lisbon, Tambora, Krakatoa and Ireland are told mostly by British observers. **Women's voices are sparse** in Constantinople, Spain and Partition. Krakatoa and Delhi have **too few convergences**.

## 2. Chapter by chapter

**Merer (c. 2560 BC).** *Gaps:* the builders' own marks. *Days:* none can be dated more finely than the year. *Add:* (1) the gang graffiti in the relieving chambers of the Great Pyramid, recorded by Vyse in 1837, which name crews such as "Friends of Khufu" (known). Set them against the crew names in Merer's papyri. (2) Khufu's rock inscriptions at Wadi Maghara in Sinai (known) and at Ayn Sukhna (check). Together they show the Red Sea and Sinai network that Wadi al-Jarf served.

**Babylon 331.** *Gaps:* the Persian and Bactrian side; mainland Greek reaction at the time. *Days:* the burning of Persepolis in spring 330 against the Athenian trial over the crown in August 330. Aeschines' *Against Ctesiphon* and Demosthenes' *On the Crown* are dated speeches that talk about Alexander's war while it was happening (known). *Add:* (1) the Aramaic documents from Bactria (Khalili collection; Naveh & Shaked 2012), which include a text dated year 1 of "Artaxerxes", meaning Bessus (known; check the exact document number). This is the usurper's own administration writing in the same months as the Diaries. (2) BCHP 3 (the Chronicle of the Successors) for the aftermath in 323 (known).

**Vesuvius 79.** *Gaps:* Jewish and provincial readings of the eruption; how the state responded. *Days:* AD 80–81, when Titus funded rebuilding. *Add:* (1) *Sibylline Oracles* Book 4, lines 130–136 (known). A Jewish text from about AD 80 reads the eruption as punishment for the destruction of the Temple in 70. (2) The Greek inscription at Naples recording Titus's rebuilding after earthquakes, IG XIV 729 (known); Martial 4.44 (known); Statius *Silvae* 4.4 (known).

**Black Death.** *Gaps:* Rus', English bishops and manors, the Jewish victims, Damascus scholars. *Days:* July 1348 in Damascus, where Ibn Battuta's account of the mass procession can be matched with Ibn Kathir's *al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya*, written in the same city (known). Late 1348, when Giovanni Villani's chronicle breaks off mid-sentence, a blank left for the date the plague would end (known). *Add:* (1) the confessions forced from Jews at Chillon and Châtel in September–October 1348, which Savoy sent on to Strasbourg (known; Horrox, *The Black Death*, 1994). Bishop Ralph of Shrewsbury's letter of January 1349 and the Walsham-le-Willows court rolls are in the same edition (known). (2) The Novgorod First Chronicle for 1352 and the death of Grand Prince Simeon in 1353 (known).

**Constantinople 1453.** *Gaps:* Ottoman documents from the time; Genoese Pera; Armenian witnesses; how the news spread. *Days:* 23 June 1453, when Lomellino, the podestà of Pera, wrote his letter (known). From 29 June to mid-July the news reached Venice, Rome and the imperial court. Aeneas Silvius Piccolomini's letter of 12 July 1453 (known) and the deliberations of the Venetian Senate (known, Archivio di Stato di Venezia) belong here. *Add:* (1) Mehmed II's victory letters (*fetihname*) to Muslim rulers (known; collected in Feridun Bey's *Münşeat*, check). (2) Armenian manuscript colophons of 1453, including Abraham of Ankara's lament (known; Sanjian, *Colophons of Armenian Manuscripts*, 1969).

**First Contact.** *Gaps:* Brazil and the Indian Ocean; conquistadors who disagreed with Cortés; the first Spanish critics of the conquest. *Days:* 21 December 1511, the Advent sermon of Antonio de Montesinos at Santo Domingo, preserved in Las Casas (known). *Add:* (1) Pero Vaz de Caminha's letter dated 1 May 1500 at Porto Seguro (known), a day-by-day account of meeting the Tupiniquim. (2) Michele da Cuneo's letter of 1495 (known); the accounts of Andrés de Tapia and Francisco de Aguilar (known); and the Codex Aubin, a Nahua annal arranged by year (known, British Museum).

**Imjin War.** *Gaps:* the court annals of all three states. The Seonjo Sillok and the Ming Shilu (Wanli reign) are both dated by day (known). Luís Fróis, *Historia de Japam* (known). *Days:* the Ming retaking of Pyongyang, 8 February 1593 (Ming 1st month, 8th day). Song Yingchang, Ryu Seong-ryong, the Sillok and Fróis all cover it. The siege of Ulsan in the winter of 1597–98, which Keinen's diary also covers (known). *Add:* (1) a convergence on Pyongyang 1593. (2) Jeong Hui-deuk's captivity diary *Wolbong haesangnok* (check), to stand beside Kang Hang's.

**Ming–Qing.** *Gaps:* Dutch, Korean and Qing court records; survivors of Jiading. *Days:* late 1644, when news of Beijing's fall reached Taiwan; the Jiading massacres of July–August 1645. *Add:* (1) the VOC *Dagregisters* kept at Zeelandia in Taiwan, which log the news from the mainland as it arrived (known; Blussé et al. edition). (2) Zhu Zisu's *Jiading tucheng jilüe*, a survivor's account (known). Also the Injo Sillok and the Shunzhi entries of the Qing Shizu Shilu (known). The diary of Ye Shaoyuan, 1645–48 (check).

**Vienna 1683.** *Gaps:* Hungarian rebels; the Venetian and French reports; how the Ottomans ended it. *Days:* 25 December 1683, when Kara Mustafa was strangled at Belgrade. Silahdar's account and the Habsburg and Venetian reports could be set side by side. *Add:* (1) Imre Thököly's correspondence (check). (2) Abraham a Sancta Clara's *Auff, auff ihr Christen!*, a dated preacher's call to arms from 1683 (known). Also the dispatches of the Venetian envoy Domenico Contarini (check).

**Lisbon 1755.** *Gaps:* almost every eye is British. Portuguese parishes, Spanish towns, Morocco and the clergy who disputed the meaning are all missing. *Days:* 1 November 1755. Fernando VI ordered a survey of more than 1,200 Spanish towns, each answering the same questions about the shock (known; printed by Martínez Solares). *Add:* (1) the *Memórias Paroquiais* of 1758, Pombal's questionnaire to every parish, held in the Torre do Tombo (known). (2) Padre Manuel Portal's manuscript *História da ruína* (known); Malagrida's *Juízo da verdadeira causa do terramoto*, 1756 (known); Rousseau's letter to Voltaire of 18 August 1756 (known).

**Tambora.** *Gaps:* the Sumbawan court's own record; Irish and Swiss famine; the Arctic. *Days:* 10–12 April 1815. The Bima court diary *Bo' Sangaji Kai* records the eruption (known; Chambert-Loir edition). *Add:* (1) the Bima entry, set beside the *Benares* report. (2) Ruprecht Zollikofer's account of the 1817 famine in eastern Switzerland (check); the post journals of the Hudson's Bay Company at York Factory (known, Archives of Manitoba); the 1817 typhus reports from Ireland (known). The Irish reports also set up the thread to the Great Hunger.

**Opium War.** *Gaps:* Qing memorials; Indian troops and Parsi traders; the British naval record. *Days:* the fall of Zhapu, 18 May 1842, where the Manchu garrison and their families killed themselves. Ouchterlony and the Qing memorials both record it. *Add:* (1) *Chouban yiwu shimo*, the dated memorials and edicts of the Daoguang reign (known). (2) Liang Tingnan's *Yifen wenji* (known); the issues of the *Chinese Repository* (known); W. D. Bernard on the *Nemesis*, 1844 (known); the letters of Jamsetjee Jejeebhoy (check).

**Irish Famine.** *Gaps:* the Poor Law unions; the Irish themselves; the transatlantic response. *Days:* the minutes of the Skibbereen Board of Guardians for December 1846, set against Cummins and Mahony. *Add:* (1) the workhouse minute books, which are dated weekly and survive for many unions (known; National Archives of Ireland and county archives). (2) The Choctaw donation of March 1847 (known); Hugh Dorian's memoir of Donegal, a late account (known). **Caution:** the "diary of Gerald Keegan" is fiction, written by Robert Sellar in 1895 (known). Robert Whyte's *The Ocean Plague* (1848) has disputed authenticity (check).

**Black Ships.** *Gaps:* the townspeople of Edo; Dutch Dejima; Ryukyu. *Days:* 8–14 July 1853. *Add:* (1) Saitō Gesshin's diary and his chronicle *Bukō nenpyō*, by an Edo townsman (known). (2) The Dejima *Dagregister* of Donker Curtius (known, Nationaal Archief); the Ryukyuan royal records of Perry's visits to Naha (check).

**Delhi 1857.** *Gaps:* the rebel government's own papers; British women on the Ridge; Awadh. *Days:* 14 September 1857. *Add:* (1) the Mutiny Papers, several thousand dated Persian and Urdu petitions and orders of the rebel administration (known, National Archives of India; used by Farooqui, *Besieged*). (2) Harriet Tytler, the only British woman on the Ridge (memoir, known). Begum Hazrat Mahal's proclamation of December 1858 (known); the proceedings of Bahadur Shah's trial, 1858 (known); the diary of Abdul Latif (check). Sitaram Pande's *From Sepoy to Subedar* has disputed authenticity (flag it as such).

**Krakatoa.** *Gaps:* Indonesian voices. *Days:* 27 August 1883, the pressure wave on barographs around the world (Symons' committee, covered). *Add:* (1) Muhammad Saleh's *Syair Lampung Karam*, a Malay poem printed in Singapore in 1883–84 (known). It is the only literary record from the region and badly needed. (2) The log of the *Charles Bal* (known); daily entries in the *Java-Bode* (check). Add a fourth convergence on the arrival of the telegraph news, 27–28 August.

**Spain 1936.** *Gaps:* women; children; a single newspaper published on both sides of the line. *Days:* 18–20 July 1936, when the Madrid *ABC* (Republican) and the Seville *ABC* (Nationalist) printed opposite accounts under the same name (known). 21–23 May 1937, the voyage of the *Habana* carrying about 4,000 Basque children to Southampton (known). *Add:* (1) the two *ABC*s as a convergence. (2) Dolores Ibárruri's dated speeches (known); Constancia de la Mora, *In Place of Splendor*, 1939 (known); Federica Montseny's papers as a minister (check).

**Moscow 1937.** *Gaps:* non-Russian peoples; scientists; letters from the accused. *Days:* 21 August 1937, the decree deporting the Soviet Koreans (known). 10 December 1937, Bukharin's letter to Stalin (known). *Add:* (1) the diaries of Mikhail Prishvin and Vladimir Vernadsky (known); Galina Shtange's diary (known; Garros et al., *Intimacy and Terror*). (2) The Korean deportation. It ties the chapter to East Asia.

**Partition 1947.** *Gaps:* Bengal; officials' reports; women's relief work; the boundary-maker himself. *Days:* 17 August 1947. *Add:* (1) the fortnightly reports of Evan Jenkins, Governor of the Punjab, and the papers of the Punjab Boundary Force (known; Mansergh, *Transfer of Power* vols X–XII). (2) Kamlaben Patel's account of recovering abducted women (known, later memoir); Radcliffe's letters to his nephew (known); the Calcutta *Statesman* under Ian Stephens (known).

**Apollo 11.** *Gaps:* protest; politicians abroad; press outside the US. *Days:* 16 July 1969, when Ralph Abernathy's Poor People's march met Thomas Paine at the Cape (known). 20–21 July, when the Italian broadcasters Tito Stagno and Ruggero Orlando disputed the moment of landing live on air (known). *Add:* (1) Tony Benn's diary for July 1969 (known); Richard Crossman's diary (known). (2) William Safire's undelivered speech "In Event of Moon Disaster", dated 18 July (known; National Archives, quotable as a US-government record).

**Wall 1989.** *Gaps:* the Stasi's own reports; the Baltic and Hungarian openings; the US–Soviet summit transcripts. *Days:* 23 August (the Baltic Way) and 19 August (the Pan-European Picnic). 2–3 December, the Malta summit, with Chernyaev's diary next to the Bush Library memcons (known). *Add:* (1) the daily mood reports of the Stasi's ZAIG unit, autumn 1989 (known, Stasi Records Archive). (2) The Bush–Kohl telephone memcons, 10 November (known, declassified).

## 3. New chapters for this volume

1. **Jerusalem Taken (1096–1100).** The First Crusade is written down by Latin, Arabic, Hebrew, Armenian and Jewish merchant hands within months. Eyes: the anonymous *Gesta Francorum* (known); Raymond of Aguilers (known); Stephen of Blois's letters to Adela, 1098 (known); Daimbert's letter to the pope, September 1099 (known); Ibn al-Qalanisi's Damascus chronicle (known); the Cairo Geniza letters on ransoming Jerusalem's Jews, 1100 (known; Goitein); Solomon bar Samson on the Rhineland massacres of 1096 (known); Matthew of Edessa (known, later).
2. **The City Burns Twice (1203–1204).** The Fourth Crusade with eyewitnesses on both sides, and a precedent for the 1453 chapter. Eyes: Villehardouin (known); Robert de Clari (known); Niketas Choniates (known); Nicholas Mesarites (known); Gunther of Pairis (known); the Novgorod First Chronicle's account of the sack (known); Innocent III's register of letters (known).
3. **Sack of Rome (1527).** Diarists, letter-writers, mercenaries and the Emperor's own spokesman all wrote within days. Eyes: Marino Sanudo's *Diarii* (known); Isabella d'Este's household letters from inside the Palazzo Colonna (known); Sebastian Schertlin von Burtenbach (known); Luigi Guicciardini (known); Alfonso de Valdés's *Diálogo* (known); Cellini (known, later memoir).
4. **Nadir Shah in Delhi (1739).** The Persian conqueror and the Mughal city, with Maratha and European agents watching. Eyes: Anand Ram Mukhlis (known; Elliot & Dowson vol. 8); Mirza Mahdi Astarabadi (known); Abd al-Karim Kashmiri, *Bayan-i Waqi'* (known); Maratha newsletters in the Peshwa Daftar (known); Basile Vatatzes's verse history of Nadir (known); East India Company letters from Bengal (check).
5. **Heavenly Capital Falls (1864).** The end of the Taiping. Eyes: Zeng Guofan's daily diary (known); Zhao Liewen's *Nengjing ju riji* (known); Li Xiucheng's deposition (known); Hong Rengan's deposition (known); Charles Gordon's letters (known); Augustus Lindley (known); the *North-China Herald* (known).
6. **Thirteen Days (October 1962).** Every capital kept minutes. Eyes: the Kennedy tapes (known, US-government); the notes taken by Malin at the Soviet Presidium (known); the Khrushchev–Kennedy letters (known, FRUS); Castro's letter of 26 October (known); Macmillan's diary (known); Kamanin's diary (known); U Thant's papers (check).
## 4. Threads

- **lisbon-1755 → first-contact.** The Casa da Índia and much of the Portuguese archive of the voyages burned in 1755 (known). Lisbon is where much of first contact's record was lost.
- **moscow-1937 → apollo-11.** Korolev and Glushko were arrested in 1938 (known). Kamanin's later diary looks back on the Terror.
- **irish-famine → delhi-1857.** Trevelyan became Governor of Madras in 1859, and Irish soldiers served in the Bengal Army.
- **imjin-war → ming-qing.** The Korean war drained the Ming treasury, and the Qing later held a Joseon crown prince hostage. Currently only ming-qing links back to imjin; add the link in the other direction.
- **tambora-1816 → irish-famine.** The Irish famine and typhus of 1816–17. Add the reverse link.
- **babylon-331 → constantinople-1453 → Vol IV.** A chain of eclipses as dated anchors (331 BC, 22 May 1453), running into Discovery.
- **krakatoa-1883 → apollo-11.** The telegraph made Krakatoa the first global news event; television made Apollo the first global live one.
## 5. Beyond this volume

- **The Sky Above (Discovery companion):** dated sky events seen in many cultures. Chapters: the supernova of 1054 (Song records, Japanese *Meigetsuki*, Ibn Butlan); Halley's Comet in 1066 (Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, Chinese records); the transit of Venus, 1769; Tunguska, 1908; the eclipse of 1919.
- **Clerks' Eyes:** a format built entirely on institutional day-books. Chapters: Merer's papyri, the Babylonian Astronomical Diaries, the Joseon Sillok, the VOC *Dagregisters*, the Stasi reports.
- **Contagion:** epidemics in many cultures. Chapters: the smallpox of 1520, cholera in 1832, the plague in Bombay in 1896, influenza in 1918, the Asian flu of 1957.
