# Review — Many Eyes, Volume VI · The Room

Reviewer stance: historian of diplomacy, religion, political thought and deliberative assemblies. Labels: **known** = confident the source exists, is dated and is published or archived; **check** = plausible, verify before use.

## Verdict

The volume picks a strong frame, the room against the people outside its door, and carries it best where the chapters lay official protocol beside private paper: Aleandro's dispatches against the Reichstag protocol, Jackson's bare Journal against Madison's notes, Mantoux against House and Nicolson, Bohlen against Maisky and Moran. The cast is varied: three Jesuits and a Jain monk at Fatehpur Sikri, Ho Chi Minh and Du Bois in Paris, Ethel Payne and Richard Wright at Bandung. The volume is thinnest in the same place in nearly every chapter. The people outside the door appear mainly as the press or as a single petitioner. Enslaved and free Black Philadelphians, Polish and Saxon subjects, colonised petitioners in 1919, Crimean locals in 1945 and African liberation movements at Bandung are all thin. The volume also has no chapter on a church council before 1521, although conciliar records are the oldest and densest "room" archive there is. And the 1700 years before Worms are left almost entirely to inference.

## Chapter by chapter

### Here I Stand? Worms 1521
**(a) Gaps.** The cities and the lesser estates are missing, though their delegates wrote home every few days:
- Frankfurt's delegate Philipp Fürstenberg, letters to the Frankfurt council (known; printed in *Deutsche Reichstagsakten, Jüngere Reihe* II, ed. Wrede 1896).
- Konrad Peutinger for Augsburg (known; letters in his *Briefwechsel*, ed. König 1923).
- The Strasbourg delegates (check).
- The Saxon chancellor Gregor Brück (known, in the Reichstagsakten).
- The emperor's confessor Jean Glapion, who opened the Ebernburg back-channel (check, dated notes of the talks).

The humanist view outside the room is missing too. Erasmus's 1521 letters (known; Allen, *Opus Epistolarum* IV) record his studied distance. Peter Martyr d'Anghiera received Valdés's report and commented on it in the *Opus epistolarum*, 1530 (known). The Wittenberg print *Acta et res gestae D. Martini Lutheri in comitiis Principum Vuormaciae* (known, 1521) shows the partisan press within weeks. A Catholic counterweight to Hutten is also needed: Johannes Cochlaeus disputed with Luther at his inn on 24 April. His *Commentaria* (1549) are retrospective, so give them a form tag (known).

**(b) Convergences.** 24 April 1521 (Trier committee, Cochlaeus's private disputation, Aleandro's report). 4 May (the staged abduction near Altenstein, plus Spalatin, Aleandro, and Dürer's Antwerp lament dated 17 May).

**(c) Additions.** Add Fürstenberg and Peutinger as two civic eyes, which turns a duel into an assembly. Add a ledger line for Luther's Wartburg letter to Melanchthon of 12 May (known).

### The House of Worship: Fatehpur Sikri
**(a) Gaps.** No woman's view. Gulbadan Begum, Akbar's aunt, led the imperial women's hajj from 1576 to 1582, which almost exactly brackets the debates. Her *Humayun-nama* (c. 1587; Beveridge trans. 1902, known) is retrospective but the only female court voice. Diplomatic correspondence is also missing. The *Insha-i Abu'l Fazl* (*Mukatabat-i Allami*) contains Akbar's letters to Abdullah Khan Uzbek, which defend his religious policy (known), and a letter to the Portuguese king (check date). The Jain side is limited to Hiravijaya. Add the Jain court-poem tradition of Padmasagara's *Jagadguru-kavya*, 1589 (check), and Shanticandra's *Kriparasakosha* (check). Both are panegyric and should be tagged as such. The Jesuit annual letters are printed in J. Correia-Afonso, *Letters from the Mughal Court* (1980, known). Monserrate's *Commentarius* (Hoyland trans. 1922, known) should be the dated backbone of the Jesuit strand. The Sikh tradition of Akbar at Goindwal has no contemporary dated record. Mention it only as tradition.

**(b) Convergences.** 3 March 1580 (Bible presentation: Monserrate, Abu'l Fazl, Badauni). The Kabul campaign, February–August 1581, is the one span where Monserrate, Abu'l Fazl, Badauni and Nizamuddin all record the same march.

**(c) Additions.** Add a Gulbadan strand, entries for departure and return. Add a short forward thread to Ralph Fitch at Fatehpur Sikri in 1585 (Hakluyt, known). Keep the Din-i Ilahi presented as three positions: Abu'l Fazl (spiritual discipleship), Badauni (apostasy), and modern historians who read it as a courtly order of loyalty.

### The Long Room: Philadelphia 1787
**(a) Gaps.** This is the most important omission in the volume. Free Black Philadelphia founded the Free African Society on 12 April 1787, under Richard Allen and Absalom Jones. Its minutes and preamble survive (known; printed in W. Douglass, *Annals of the First African Church*, 1862). The Pennsylvania Abolition Society minute books are at the Historical Society of Pennsylvania (known). The enslaved men Washington brought to Philadelphia appear in his household accounts (check: names and dates in the *Papers of George Washington*).

The city's own record is missing. Elizabeth Drinker's diary (ed. Crane 1991, known; check how much it notes delegates). Jacob Hiltzheimer's diary, which logs Washington and delegates (known, *PMHB* 1892 extracts).

So are foreign observers:
- French chargé Louis-Guillaume Otto, dispatches to Montmorin (known; partly in *Documentary History of the Ratification*).
- British consul Phineas Bond, letters to the Foreign Office (known; *AHA Annual Report* 1896–97).
- Gardoqui in New York (known).

Further delegates' notes survive: John Dickinson's and William Samuel Johnson's diary (known; Hutson, *Supplement to Farrand's Records*, 1987).

**(b) Convergences.** 13 July 1787: the Confederation Congress in New York passed the Northwest Ordinance, with its slavery ban and Indian "good faith" clause, while the Convention sat. That gives two rooms on one day. 17 September (Washington, Madison, McHenry, Franklin, Hiltzheimer).

**(c) Additions.** Add the Free African Society and Otto. With them, the chapter's "outside the room" has a Black Philadelphian and a foreign eye, not only Cutler.

### The Congress Dances: Vienna 1814–1815
**(a) Gaps.** The objects of the bargaining are silent:
- Poles: Adam Jerzy Czartoryski, Alexander's adviser, letters and memoranda (known; *Mémoires et correspondance*, 1887).
- Saxons: the captive king's envoys (check).
- Swiss: Charles Pictet de Rochemont (known; correspondence published 1892).
- Hanseatic cities: Johann Smidt for Bremen (known).
- Jewish communities: the Frankfurt and Hanseatic Jewish deputations; C. A. Buchholz, agent for Lübeck/Bremen Jewry (check).

German national opinion is also missing. The Freiherr vom Stein's Vienna diary (known; *Briefe und amtliche Schriften* V) is essential. Women hold the salons the intro mentions but have no voice. The Metternich–Wilhelmine of Sagan letters (ed. Ullrichová 1966, known) are dated and political. Fanny von Arnstein's salon appears in Hager's reports. The Hager reports themselves should be credited to Fournier, *Die Geheimpolizei auf dem Wiener Kongress* (1913) and Weil, *Les dessous du Congrès* (1917) (both known). The Ottoman absence (the Porte declined a guarantee) deserves a ledger line.

**(b) Convergences.** 29 November 1814: Beethoven's *Der glorreiche Augenblick* concert before the sovereigns. Gentz, Hager's agents and the *Wiener Zeitung* all record it.

**(c) Additions.** Add Stein and Czartoryski. They make the Saxony–Poland crisis a contest of the governed as well as the governors.

### The Wesleyan Chapel: Seneca Falls 1848
**(a) Gaps.** The Haudenosaunee context is missing. Mott visited the Cattaraugus Seneca in the weeks before the convention. Her letters describe Seneca women's standing (check; *Selected Letters of Lucretia Coffin Mott*, ed. Palmer 2002, known as an edition). The Seneca Nation adopted its republican constitution in December 1848 (known). Working women are reduced to Charlotte Woodward. Add Sarah Bagley and the Lowell Female Labor Reform Association (known; *Voice of Industry*). The international 1848 is missing: Jeanne Deroin and Eugénie Niboyet's *La Voix des Femmes* (Paris, March–June 1848, known). Opponents need a voice beyond newspapers: local clergy sermons (check). For 1840, add Benjamin Robert Haydon's diary of painting the Anti-Slavery Convention, including his sitting with Mott (known; Pope ed., vol. IV) and Anne Knight's letters (known, Friends House Library).

**(b) Convergences.** 12–17 June 1840 (Mott diary, Haydon, Garrison letters). 7 April 1848 (New York Married Women's Property Act) belongs in the ledger.

**(c) Additions.** Add one Seneca or Paris 1848 strand to break the chapter's purely Anglo-American frame.

### Paris 1919
**(a) Gaps.** Several voices are missing:
- Korea: Kim Kyu-sik's petition after the March First Movement (known).
- Egypt: Saad Zaghlul's diaries, *Mudhakkirat* (known, published Cairo).
- Zionists: Weizmann letters, 27 February hearing (known).
- Armenians: Boghos Nubar and Aharonian (known).
- Italy's walk-out: Aldrovandi Marescotti's diary (known, 1936–38).
- The French president: Poincaré's diary, *Au service de la France* XI (known).
- The secretary in the Four: Hankey's diary, Churchill Archives (known).
- Women: the Inter-Allied Women's Conference, February–April 1919 (known).
- Labour: Gompers's Commission on International Labour Legislation, minutes in FRUS (known).
- The press chief: Ray Stannard Baker's diary, LC (known).

**(b) Convergences.** 4 May 1919: Beijing students march over Shandong four days after the award (Koo, Grayson, Chinese press). 24 April (Orlando leaves; Wilson's Fiume statement).

**(c) Additions.** Add the May Fourth strand and Zaghlul. Together they show that the decisions made outside the room started movements.

### Yalta 1945
**(a) Gaps.** The Soviet official record is missing: *Sovetskii Soyuz na mezhdunarodnykh konferentsiyakh* vol. IV (1979, known). So is the military diary trio: Alanbrooke (Danchev & Todman 2001, known), Leahy (LC, known) and Stettinius's calendar notes (Campbell & Herring 1975, known). Poland outside has only Arciszewski. Add Edward Raczyński's diary, *In Allied London* (known). The other parties to the Far East bargain were absent. Add Chiang Kai-shek's diary at the Hoover Institution (known; check February entries). On the Japanese side, Prince Konoe presented his memorial to the emperor on 14 February 1945 (known; Kido diary). The Crimean ground is missing: the Crimean Tatars had been deported in May 1944, and Beria's NKVD reports covered the conference security (check). Present the "betrayal" versus "best available bargain" argument as positions.

**(b) Convergences.** 13–14 February 1945: the London Polish declaration, Dresden (Klemperer diary, known), and Goebbels's diary reaction to the communiqué (known).

**(c) Additions.** Add Chiang and Konoe. They extend the room to the Pacific, which the secret agreement was about.

### Bandung 1955
**(a) Gaps.** Africa is under-weighted. Add the Gold Coast delegate Kojo Botsio (check), the ANC observers Moses Kotane and Maulvi Cachalia (known as attendees; check for dated reports), the Algerian FLN's Hocine Aït Ahmed and M'hamed Yazid (known attendees), and Makarios for Cyprus (known). The host press is missing: Mochtar Lubis in *Indonesia Raya* (known). Chinese Foreign Ministry files are now available through the Wilson Center Digital Archive (known). Add *Selected Works of Jawaharlal Nehru*, 2nd ser. vol. 28 (known), and Carl Rowan's dispatches (known). Roeslan Abdulgani's *The Bandung Connection* is retrospective and should be tagged as such (known).

**(b) Convergences.** 22–23 April (bloc debate, Zhou's offer, Dulles's reply).

**(c) Additions.** Add one African liberation-movement eye, plus the Wilson Center Chinese files. The latter turn Zhou from a performer into a dated planner.

## New chapters for this volume

1. **The Council of Constance, 1414–1418.**
   - Why it fits: it is the first great assembly with competing diarists, and the trial of Hus is set against the ending of the Schism. It also gives Worms its precedent.
   - Eyes:
     - Cardinal Guillaume Fillastre, diary (known; Finke, *Acta Concilii Constanciensis* II)
     - Jacopo Cerretani, curial diary (known)
     - Ulrich Richental, burgher chronicle (known)
     - Peter of Mladoňovice, eyewitness account of Hus (known)
     - Jan Hus, prison letters (known)
     - Poggio Bracciolini, letter on Jerome of Prague, 1416 (known)
2. **The Council of Florence, 1438–1439.**
   - Why it fits: Greek and Latin churches in one room, with each side's record contradicting the other's.
   - Eyes:
     - Sylvester Syropoulos, memoirs (known; Laurent 1971)
     - the Greek *Acta* (known)
     - Andrea da Santacroce's Latin acts (known)
     - Bessarion's letters (known)
     - Ambrogio Traversari's letters (known)
     - the Ethiopian delegation of 1441 (check for its records)
3. **Westphalia, 1643–1648.**
   - Why it fits: two congress towns and dozens of delegations; edited diaries exist for many of them.
   - Eyes:
     - Fabio Chigi, nuncio, diary (known; *Acta Pacis Westphalicae*)
     - Isaak Volmar, *Diarium* (known, APW)
     - Johan Adler Salvius, Swedish correspondence (known, APW)
     - Alvise Contarini, Venetian mediator (known)
     - Adam Adami, Benedictine delegate's *Relatio* (known)
     - the Dutch delegation's reports (check)
4. **The Hague Peace Conference, 1899.**
   - Why it fits: the first world disarmament conference, with women and the press camped outside.
   - Eyes:
     - Bertha von Suttner, *Die Haager Friedensconferenz: Tagebuchblätter* (known, 1900)
     - Andrew Dickson White's diary in his *Autobiography* (known)
     - Fyodor Martens's diary (check)
     - W. T. Stead's dispatches (known)
     - Siamese, Japanese and Ottoman delegates' reports (check)
     - the official *Proceedings* (known)
5. **Round Table Conference, London, 1931.**
   - Why it fits: Gandhi, Ambedkar, the princes and the communal delegations, deadlocked over minority representation.
   - Eyes:
     - Mahadev Desai's diary (known)
     - B. R. Ambedkar's writings and speeches (known)
     - the Aga Khan (check)
     - Sarojini Naidu's letters (known)
     - the official *Proceedings* (known)
     - Samuel Hoare's papers (check)
6. **San Francisco, April–June 1945.**
   - Why it fits: fifty nations write the UN Charter, and a small coalition of women forces equality language into it.
   - Eyes:
     - Arthur Vandenberg's diary (known)
     - Bertha Lutz's report and notes (check)
     - Virginia Gildersleeve (known; memoir is retrospective)
     - Wellington Koo's diary, Columbia (known)
     - Stettinius (known)
     - Jan Smuts's letters (known)
     - Soviet delegation telegrams (check)
7. **The Second Vatican Council, 1962–1965.**
   - Why it fits: rich theologians' diaries and non-Catholic observers in the room.
   - Eyes:
     - Yves Congar, *Mon journal du Concile* (known)

## Threads

1. **Constance (new) → Worms.** On 18 April Luther asserted that councils have erred, meaning Constance's condemnation of Hus.
2. **Vienna 1815 → Yalta.** Poland's frontiers and government were settled by great powers in both rooms; Czartoryski and Arciszewski are matching outsiders.
3. **Paris 1919 Shandong → Yalta Far East agreement.** China's rights were bargained without China present both times, and Koo was on hand in 1945 at San Francisco.
4. **Philadelphia's slave-trade week → Vienna's slave-trade declaration → London 1840 (Seneca Falls prologue).** Three rooms deal with the same question across 53 years.
5. **Ho Chi Minh, Paris 1919 → Pham Van Dong, Bandung 1955.** The petitioner of 1919 led a government that sent a delegation in 1955.
6. **Seneca Falls → Inter-Allied Women's Conference, Paris 1919 → San Francisco 1945.** Women push their way into conference texts.
7. **Fatehpur Sikri → Vol. III exploration.** Jesuits and Ralph Fitch (1585) on the road to the Mughal court.
8. **Westphalia (new) → Vol. I Thirty Years' War.** The war's end, seen from the negotiating table.

## Beyond this volume

- **The Courtroom**: trials as rooms with many hostile witnesses. Joan of Arc 1431; Galileo 1633; Salem 1692; Warren Hastings 1788–95; Dreyfus 1894–99.
- **The Treaty Ground**: councils between states and Indigenous nations, with both sides' records. Waitangi 1840; Fort Laramie 1851; Medicine Lodge 1867; Treaty 6 1876; Lancaster 1744.
- **The Square** (companion format): the crowd outside the room, chapter by chapter. May Fourth 1919; Kennington Common 1848; the Paris June Days 1848.
