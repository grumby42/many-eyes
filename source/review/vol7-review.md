# Review: Many Eyes, Volume VII: Getting There

"Known" means confident the source exists and is dated; "check" means plausible, verify first. Web lookups confirmed the 1929 Graf Zeppelin observers (airships.net list, from Geisenheyner's book) and John Adams's 1783 balloon diary (MHS, Adams Papers, Diary vol. 3); the rest is my own knowledge.

## Verdict

The volume handles the arc from idea to trial to mastery well, and its ledgers are disciplined: figures as ranges, conflicts named, positions kept apart from facts (Nares, Goddard's Mach claim, the Hammondsport hops). Rainhill, Kitty Hawk and Airships are richest because their sources are plural and dated. It is thinnest in three places: it is overwhelmingly North Atlantic (only Shinkansen and half of Oceans lie elsewhere); workers and passengers appear as collective "records" rather than named writers; and 1897–1906 on the road and 1958–1968 in the jet age are near-empty. The fix: one named worker or passenger and one non-Western press or state eye per chapter, plus chapters set in Asia, the Middle East and Latin America.

## Chapter by chapter

### Lighter Than Air (balloons-1783)
- **Gaps.** The chapter has no Paris street diarist and no women.
  - Paris diarists: Siméon-Prosper Hardy, *Mes loisirs*, the daily journal of a Paris bookseller (BnF ms. fr. 6680–6687; critical edition Hermann, 2012–) (known). The *Mémoires secrets* (Bachaumont continuators), dated entries (known). The duc de Croÿ, *Journal inédit* (ed. Grouchy & Cottin, 1906–07), who watched the 1783 ascents (known). John Adams, Diary vol. 3, on the Paris balloons of 1783 (known). John Quincy Adams, then aged 16 in Paris, in his diary (check).
  - Women: Élisabeth Thible at Lyon on 4 June 1784, the first woman aloft (known as an event; her sources are press and later accounts). Letitia Sage, the first Englishwoman aloft (29 June 1785), who printed *A Letter Addressed to a Female Friend* (1785) (known).
  - English provincial witnesses: Gilbert White, *Naturalist's Journal* and letters, on Blanchard's balloon passing near Selborne on 16 October 1784 (known). William Cowper's letters on "air balloons," 1783–84 (known). Samuel Johnson's letters on balloons, autumn 1784 (known).
- **Densest days.**
  - 1 December 1783: Hardy, Croÿ, Mémoires secrets, Franklin to Banks, Adams.
  - 15 September 1784: Lunardi's printed *Account* (letters to Chevalier Compagni, 1784, known), Walpole, the London papers.
  - 19 January 1784, Le Flesselles at Lyon with seven aboard, is missing altogether.
- **Additions.** Make Hardy the "crowd" eye across all the 1783 convergences. Add Letitia Sage to break the all-male aeronaut list.

### Steam on the Water (steam-water)
- **Gaps.**
  - The western rivers: the *New Orleans* (1811) ran through the New Madrid earthquakes with Lydia Latrobe Roosevelt aboard, known only via J. H. B. Latrobe's 1871 retelling (known; late). Eliza Bryan's dated earthquake letter in Lorenzo Dow's journal (known) is contemporary.
  - There are no stokers or deck crew. The *Savannah*'s log, kept by sailing master Stevens Rogers, is held at the Smithsonian / National Museum of American History (check custodian). Swedish and Russian port reports from her 1819 Stockholm and St Petersburg calls (check).
  - Steam's first long eastern voyage is missing: the *Enterprize*, Falmouth to Calcutta in 1825. It is recorded in the Calcutta press and East India Company correspondence, British Library IOR (known).
- **Densest days.** 23 April 1838: Hone, Bennett's *Herald*, Hosken, Roberts, plus the *Courier and Enquirer* and the *New-York American* (known titles).
- **Additions.** Add the *New Orleans* in 1811 as a western counter-scene. Add a Calcutta eye for 1825.

### Rainhill and the Opening Day (rainhill-1830)
- **Gaps.**
  - The losing side and the opposition. John Ericsson's and John Braithwaite's letters on Novelty (check). The canal interests: Bridgewater Trustees' papers (check archive). Charles Vignoles's diaries, extracted in O. J. Vignoles, *Life of Charles Blacker Vignoles* (1889) (known).
  - Manchester's hostile crowd on opening day: the *Manchester Guardian* of 18 September 1830 (known) and *The Times* (known).
  - The company's own minutes: The National Archives RAIL 371 (known).
  - The Atlantic echo: Rastrick also built the *Stourbridge Lion*, run at Honesdale, Pennsylvania, on 8 August 1829 by Horatio Allen. It is recorded in the Delaware & Hudson Canal Company records (known).
- **Densest days.** 15 September 1830, which could carry about 10 eyes if the Manchester press and Lord Wilton's tourniquet account (through Creevey and Greville) are added. 6–8 October 1829, with *The Times* and the Liverpool Courier alongside the Mechanics' Magazine.
- **Additions.** An opposition eye (Manchester radicals, canal men); the *Stourbridge Lion* as a one-entry aside.

### Joining the Oceans and the Continent (oceans-1869)
- **Gaps.** Chinese workers are rightly handled through payrolls; cite the Stanford Chinese Railroad Workers in North America Project (known). Missing on the line:
  - Brigham Young's office journal and the Mormon grading contracts (Church History Library, Salt Lake City) (known).
  - Alfred A. Hart, the Central Pacific's photographer, with dated negatives (known).
  - Native nations: the Plum Creek derailment by Turkey Leg's Cheyenne in August 1867, seen through UP telegrams and the Cheyenne accounts George Bird Grinnell collected later (known; label as late).
- Missing at Suez:
  - Empress Eugénie's letters to Napoleon III from Egypt, in *Lettres familières de l'impératrice Eugénie* (1935) (known).
  - Franz Joseph's letters to Elisabeth from the 1869 journey (ed. Nostitz-Rieneck, 1966) (known).
  - Thomas Cook's first Nile party (*Cook's Excursionist*, 1869) (known).
  - Emir Abd el-Kader's presence (known as an event; his letters need checking).
  - An Egyptian press eye: *Wādī al-Nīl* (check coverage).
  - Ali Mubarak, as an Egyptian official (check).
- **Densest days.** 17 November 1869: Eugénie, Franz Joseph, the Crown Prince, Ibsen, Fromentin, de Lesseps and Cook could make seven eyes in one day.
- **Additions.** Add Eugénie and Franz Joseph. Add Brigham Young to give Promontory a local, non-company voice.

### The Road (the-road)
- **Gaps.** There are no entries between 1897 and 1906.
  - Horatio Nelson Jackson's first US transcontinental drive (May–July 1903), told in dated letters to his wife Bertha (the published letters exist; check the holding archive).
  - Paris–Madrid, 24 May 1903, when Marcel Renault and others were killed and the race was stopped at Bordeaux (French press, known).
  - Henry Ford's Quadricycle, 4 June 1896 (Benson Ford Research Center) (known).
  - Riders outside the West: Thomas Stevens, *Around the World on a Bicycle* (1887, from dated *Outing* dispatches, 1884–86) (known). Fanny Bullock Workman and William Hunter Workman's dated cycle-travel books on Algeria, Spain and India (1895–1900) (known).
  - In Peking–Paris, everyone is European. Add Qing permissions and Russian telegraph reports (check). Charles Godard of the Spyker (check).
- **Densest days.** 10 June 1907, Peking departure: Barzini, du Taillis, Borghese, plus the *North-China Herald* (known title; check coverage).
- **Additions.** Add Jackson in 1903 and Paris–Madrid in 1903 to close the gap and add a second fatal turning point.

### Kitty Hawk (kitty-hawk)
- **Gaps.**
  - The local and federal record: the Kill Devil Hills Life-Saving Station logbooks (National Archives, RG 26) (known). The Weather Bureau's Kitty Hawk station and observer Joseph Dosher, who passed the telegram (known). Addie Tate (check).
  - Correspondents: George Spratt's correspondence with Wilbur (Library of Congress, Wright Papers) (known). Octave Chanute's own papers (LoC) (known).
  - The French reception: Ferdinand Ferber's letters and *L'Aérophile* (known title).
  - Scepticism: the *New York Times* editorial "Flying Machines Which Do Not Fly," 10 October 1903 (known).
  - The Whitehead claim, as a position.
- **Densest days.** 17 December 1903, with the station log, the Weather Bureau record and Milton Wright's diary added: about 8 eyes. 8 December 1903, with the Washington press.
- **Additions.** The station log and Weather Bureau record: dated and independent of the Wrights.

### Across the Channel and the Atlantic (channel-atlantic)
- **Gaps.** The South Atlantic and the Iberian, Italian and Latin American flyers are missing:
  - Gago Coutinho and Sacadura Cabral, Lisbon to Rio, March–June 1922 (Coutinho's navigation records; Portuguese and Brazilian press) (known).
  - Ramón Franco's *Plus Ultra*, 1926 (known).
  - Francesco de Pinedo, 1927 (known).
  - João Ribeiro de Barros's *Jahú*, 1927 (known).
- Also missing: the Clifden Marconi station staff on 15 June 1919 (check), and the Paris press's false report that Nungesser and Coli had arrived (*La Presse*, 9–10 May 1927) (known as an episode).
- **Densest days.** 21–22 May 1927: Lindbergh, Herrick, the Paris dailies, the *New York Times*, and Coolidge's cable (known).
- **Additions.** Add the 1922 South Atlantic crossing as its own convergence. Add the Nungesser–Coli false-news day as the French side of Lindbergh.

### Airships (airships)
- **Gaps.**
  - Japan, confirmed: the 1929 world flight carried Lt Cdr Fujiyoshi, Lt Cdr Kusaka Ryūnosuke, Major Shibata, a Soviet representative (Karklin), Jerónimo Megías (physician to Alfonso XIII) and reporters for the *Asahi*, the *Mainichi* and Nippon Denpo. The *Asahi* and *Mainichi* coverage of Kasumigaura on 19 August is known. Megías's book (check). Rosendahl, *Up Ship!* (1931) (check).
  - Italy and the USSR: the *Norge* (1926) and *Italia* (1928) flights. Nobile's accounts. The Soviet radio amateur Nikolai Schmidt picked up the *Italia* SOS on 3 June 1928. The icebreaker *Krasin*'s rescue on 12 July. Amundsen was lost on 18 June (all known).
  - Zeppelin-raid victims: Mass Observation postdates the raids, so use Virginia Woolf's diary, 1917–18 (known), and local inquests.
  - R101: Lord Thomson's letters to Princess Marthe Bibesco (known). The Allonne villagers' testimony to the Simon inquiry (known).
- **Densest days.** 25 May 1928 and 12 July 1928, if the *Italia* is added. 19 August 1929 at Kasumigaura.
- **Additions.** The *Italia* sub-arc; Japanese press and officers at Kasumigaura.

### Rockets (rockets)
- **Gaps.**
  - The victims and the enslaved: the Dora (Nordhausen) war-crimes trial record of 1947 (NARA, US Army; quotable as a US-government record) (known). Jean Michel, *Dora* (1975; flag it as late) (known). Mass Observation diarists on the V-2s from 8 September 1944 (University of Sussex) (known). The Antwerp civil records (check).
  - Soviet voices: Tsiolkovsky's letters, Glushko and Tikhonravov (check editions). Korolev's prison letters, via Natalya Koroleva's biography (known; late).
  - Asia: Itokawa Hideo's pencil rocket, Kokubunji, April 1955 (known). Qian Xuesen's deportation to China in 1955 (known). Thumba, 21 November 1963 (Vikram Sarabhai) (known), which fits the stated 1969 outer limit.
- **Densest days.** 4–5 October 1957: TASS, *Pravda* (5 October, a small item), the *NYT*, Moonwatch, Jodrell Bank / Bernard Lovell (known), Eisenhower's press conference of 9 October (US-government record, quotable).
- **Additions.** Add the Dora trial testimony; the V-2 entry needs a victim's voice, not only a death toll. Add Itokawa or Thumba.

### Faster Than Sound and the Jet Age (jet-age)
- **Gaps.**
  - Passengers and cabin crew are absent. BOAC stewardesses and the Elba recovery could be told through Royal Navy and Italian fishermen's reports to the Cohen court (known).
  - Women: Jacqueline Cochran's Mach 1 on 18 May 1953 (known) and Jacqueline Auriol (known).
  - The failure that preceded Yeager: Geoffrey de Havilland Jr, killed in the DH 108 on 27 September 1946 (known).
  - The 1958–1968 gap: the Tu-104 at Heathrow, 22 March 1956 (British press). Air India's first 707 service, 1960 (check date). The Oklahoma City sonic-boom tests of 1964 (FAA records, known; US-government, quotable).
  - The anti-Concorde campaigners: Richard Wiggs's Anti-Concorde Project, 1966–67 (known).
- **Densest days.** 2 March and 9 April 1969: Turcat, Trubshaw, Benn's diary, Flight, Hansard.
- **Additions.** The Oklahoma City boom records (the public's voice, quotable) and Cochran.

### The Bullet Train (shinkansen)
- **Gaps.**
  - The Diet: National Diet Library's Kokkai Kaigiroku minutes database (known).
  - The press: *Asahi*, *Yomiuri* and NHK coverage of opening day (known titles).
  - Landowners and construction workers: right-of-way disputes and construction deaths in JNR construction histories (check). The "three great follies" jibe (Great Wall, pyramids, battleship *Yamato*), which belongs in the sceptics' voice (known as a phrase; trace a dated source).
  - Foreign press beyond Time and UPI: *The Times*, *Le Monde* (known titles).
  - The Olympic link: the Games opened on 10 October 1964.
- **Densest days.** 1 October 1964: Ishida, the Emperor, JNR, Time, UPI, Asahi and the Diet.
- **Additions.** Add Diet minutes for the 1963 cost overrun, and one opening-day passenger or crew account (check JNR oral histories).

## New chapters for this volume

1. **Railways come to India, 1853–1870.** Coloniser, colonised and a distant theorist, dated within weeks. Eyes: Dalhousie's Railway Minute, 20 Apr 1853 (known); *Bombay Times* on the Bombay–Thane train, 16 Apr 1853 (known); Jagannath Shankarseth, GIPR director (minutes; check); Marx, "The Future Results of British Rule in India," *NYDT*, 8 Aug 1853 (known); Bholanath Chunder, *Travels of a Hindoo* (1869) (known); Lady Canning's letters (known).
2. **The Wusong Railway, 1876–1877.** China's first line, built by a foreign firm, bought by the Qing and torn up. Eyes: *Shenbao* (known); *North-China Herald* (known); Jardine Matheson papers, Cambridge UL (known); Li Hongzhang and Shen Baozhen memorials (check editions).
3. **Railway Time, 1883–1884.** The "day of two noons," 18 Nov 1883, is in hundreds of local papers. Eyes: Sandford Fleming's diaries, LAC (known); William F. Allen's General Time Convention papers (check); *International Meridian Conference Protocols*, 1884, US government (known); Naval Observatory records (check).
4. **The Trans-Siberian, 1890–1904.** Eyes: Tsarevich Nicholas's diary, Vladivostok, 31 May 1891 (known); Chekhov's 1890 Siberian letters (known); Witte's memoirs (known; late); Annette Meakin, *A Ribbon of Iron* (1901) (known); Chinese Eastern Railway and convict-labour records (check).
5. **The Hejaz Railway and the steam Hajj, 1900–1908.** Eyes: Ottoman press, *İkdam* (check); Meissner Pasha's papers (check); al-Batanuni, *al-Rihla al-Hijaziyya* (1910) (known); British consular reports from Jeddah, TNA FO 195 (known). Opening at Medina, 1 Sep 1908.
6. **The Panama Canal, 1904–1914.** Eyes: *The Canal Record*, US government weekly (known); Isthmian Canal Commission reports (known); Goethals's and Gorgas's letters (known); West Indian workers' testimonies from the Isthmian Historical Society's 1963 competition (known; late, flag it); Panamanian press (check). *Ancon* transits 15 Aug 1914.
7. **The MacRobertson Air Race, October 1934.** England to Melbourne via Baghdad, Allahabad, Singapore and Darwin. Eyes: KLM *Uiver* crew and passengers (check logs); Albury press after the midnight landing (known); Scott and Campbell Black (press); Indian and Malayan press (check).
8. **The Berlin Airlift, 1948–1949.** Eyes: Ruth Andreas-Friedrich's diary (known); USAF and RAF operations records (known; US records quotable); Soviet *Tägliche Rundschau* (known title); Gail Halvorsen (memoir; late); Berlin magistrate records (check).
