## Ash on the Wind (1618–1649) [id thirty-years]
Intro: The Thirty Years' War began with a political gesture in Prague in May 1618, when Protestant Bohemian nobles threw two imperial governors and a secretary out of a castle window. It ended in October 1648 with two treaties negotiated at Münster and Osnabrück and signed at Münster. In the thirty years between, armies of Germans, Swedes, Spaniards, Croats, Scots, Walloons, Italians and Finns marched back and forth across central Europe. They were paid badly or not at all, and they fed themselves by taking food from farms and towns. Several million people died, more from hunger and plague than from fighting. Nobody living through it saw it as one war. A shoemaker near Ulm saw comets, inflation, plague and soldiers coming down the road again. ## The Bills, the Bonfire and the Fire (1665–1666) [id london-1666]
Intro: In the summer of 1665 London began to die by the printed page. Every Thursday the parish clerks' Bills of Mortality reported the week's burials, and across England people copied the numbers: 112, then 1,089, then over 6,000 of plague alone in a week. The court fled to Salisbury and Oxford; the rich followed in coaches. Those who stayed — officials, clergy, the poor — lived among red crosses on doors, dead-carts and empty streets. Perhaps a hundred thousand Londoners, nearly a quarter of the city, died in eighteen months. All the while England was at war with the Dutch Republic, its great rival for trade.
