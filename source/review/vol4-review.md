# Review: Many Eyes, Volume IV · The Notebook

Known = confident the source exists and is dated; check = plausible, verify first. Three web searches were inconclusive, so those items stay "check".

## 1. Verdict

The volume gets the form right. It treats patent minutes, anagrams, plis cachetés, Comptes rendus sitting dates and dated typescripts as the real "diaries" of discovery, and it says so honestly in the form tags. That makes the priority ledgers its strongest feature. The Edo chapter is the best in the volume: it names the outcaste dissector who left no writing, and it reads the Dejima Dagregisters against Thunberg. Elsewhere the volume is thinnest in three places. It gives little space to **sceptics and rival claimants** (Matthew, Scheiner, Fabricius, Paulescu-type figures). It has almost no **non-European scientific eyes** outside Japan. And **medicine** is limited to one anatomy chapter, in a volume titled "Discovery". The last three chapters (Curies, fission, double helix) also stay inside one Western European elite network and leave out technicians, assistants and the press.

## 2. Chapter by chapter

### The Telescope Turned Skyward (1608–1611)

**(a) Depth gaps.**
- **The Siamese embassy is in the intro but not among the eyes.** The French pamphlet *Ambassades du Roy de Siam envoyé à l'Excellence du Prince Maurice* (The Hague, 1608) is the first printed news of the telescope (known). It belongs in the ledger.
- **Diplomats.** Pierre Jeannin, Henri IV's negotiator at the truce, wrote to the king about the spyglass in late December 1608. It appears in his *Négociations* (known; check the exact date).
- **Paris street view.** Pierre de L'Estoile's *Journal* has spyglasses on sale on the Pont Neuf by 30 April 1609 (known).
- **French observers.** Nicolas-Claude Fabri de Peiresc and Joseph Gaultier de la Valette observed the Jovian moons at Aix in November 1610. Peiresc's papers are at the Bibliothèque Inguimbertine, Carpentras (known; check the exact nights).
- **The sunspot rivals are missing. This is the chapter's real priority gap.** Johannes Fabricius, *De maculis in sole observatis* (Wittenberg, dedication dated 13 June 1611) (known). Christoph Scheiner SJ, the "Apelles" letters to Marcus Welser dated 12 November, 19 December and 26 December 1611 (known).
- **Patron correspondence.** Galileo's letters to Belisario Vinta are in Favaro's *Edizione Nazionale*, vol. X (known).
- **Naming the instrument.** The Lincei met at Federico Cesi's on 14 April 1611, the evening the instrument was named *telescopium*. Johannes Demisiani is credited with the name (known).
- **Beyond Europe.** John Saris's journal records his gift of a telescope to Tokugawa Ieyasu in 1613 (known; Hakluyt Society edition). Manuel Dias's *Tianwen lüe* (Beijing, 1615) is the first Chinese account of the telescope (known).

**(b) Unexploited convergences.** Late November 1610: Peiresc, Harriot, Galileo and Kepler were all working on Jupiter. 14 April 1611: the Lincei banquet, with Galileo's demonstration and the naming. June to December 1611: sunspots, with Fabricius, Scheiner, Harriot and Galileo on overlapping days.

**(c) Additions.** 1. Add a short "sunspot priority" coda (1611–1613) with Scheiner and Fabricius as eyes. 2. Make the Siamese embassy pamphlet the October 1608 opener. That gives the chapter a non-European frame it currently only gestures at.

### A Black Drop on the Sun: The Transits of Venus (1761–1769)

**(a) Depth gaps.**
- **Tahitian view.** The Endeavour's own journals give it, through Tupaia and Purea. Joseph Banks's *Endeavour Journal* (Beaglehole ed., 1962) (known) and Sydney Parkinson's *Journal* (1773) (known) cover 3 June 1769. Banks was at the Moorea outstation that day. Daniel Solander and Herman Spöring are further Endeavour hands (known).
- **Partners left out.** Jeremiah Dixon, Mason's partner at the Cape in 1761 (known). Joseph Dymond, Wales's partner at Churchill (known).
- **Hudson's Bay Company.** The Prince of Wales Fort post journal for 1768–69 is in the HBC Archives, Archives of Manitoba, series B.42/a (known). Governor Moses Norton's official view sits alongside Wales.
- **Colonial and non-European stations.** Johan Maurits Mohr observed both transits at Batavia (known). José Antonio Alzate observed in Mexico City in 1769 (known). Vicente de Doz and Salvador de Medina were with Chappe in Baja California (known). Augustin Hallerstein SJ and the Jesuits of the Beijing Astronomical Bureau observed in 1769 (check).
- **Other 1761 stations.** John Winthrop of Harvard at St John's, Newfoundland, 1761 (known). Christian Mayer SJ at St Petersburg in 1769, at Catherine II's invitation (known).
- **A woman computer.** Nicole-Reine Lepaute, Lalande's collaborator (check whether her transit work is dated).

**(b) Unexploited convergences.** 4 June 1769, the day after the transit: a solar eclipse was timed at many of the same stations, including Tahiti, Norriton, Vardø and Santa Ana. For 1761, the Batavia, Tobolsk, Selenginsk and Rodrigues timings could all sit on one Asian-longitude row.

**(c) Additions.** 1. Add Banks or Parkinson plus Tupaia (as reported) at Point Venus. 2. Add the HBC post journal next to Wales and Dymond. Both bring in local and "worker" vantage points without inventing any.

### The Body on the Execution Ground (1754–1815)

**(a) Depth gaps.** The chapter is already strong. What it lacks:
- **The later reckoning.** Ōtsuki Gentaku's *Rangaku kaitei* (1788) and his *Jūtei Kaitai Shinsho* (1826) (known).
- **Sugita's own diary.** The *Itsusai nichiroku* (check whether edited and dated).
- **Edo witnesses to Dutch learning.** Shiba Kōkan, *Saiyū nikki*, a dated travel diary of 1788 (known).
- **The Dutch side after Thunberg.** Isaac Titsingh, opperhoofd 1779–84, and his correspondence with Kutsuki Masatsuna (known).
- **The Kyoto counter-tradition.** Kawaguchi Shinnin's *Kaishihen* (1772), a Kyoto dissection that kept Chinese categories (known).
- **The Nagasaki interpreters,** whose Dutch was the real bottleneck. Yoshio Kōgyū and Motoki Ryōei (known as people; check for dated writings).
- **A Chinese comparator.** Wang Qingren's *Yilin gaicuo* (1830). It was based on corpses seen in the 1790s and is a striking parallel in which a physician corrects classical anatomy by looking (known).

**(b) Unexploited convergences.** Spring 1776 in Edo: Thunberg's journal, Feith's Dagregister, and Hiraga Gennai's working *erekiteru* (restored that year).

**(c) Additions.** 1. Add Ōtsuki Gentaku as the "second generation" voice, which also tests Sugita's 1815 credit story. 2. Add Wang Qingren as a coda thread on non-European anatomy reform.

### Two Notebooks, One Theory (1837–1860)

**(a) Depth gaps.**
- **The missing rival priority claim.** Patrick Matthew's letter in the *Gardeners' Chronicle* of 7 April 1860, and Darwin's reply on 21 April 1860 (known).
- **Lyell's own species doubts.** His species notebooks, *Sir Charles Lyell's Scientific Journals on the Species Question* (Wilkinson ed., 1970) (known).
- **Sceptics.** Adam Sedgwick to Darwin, 24 November 1859 (known; Darwin Correspondence Project). Richard Owen's anonymous *Edinburgh Review* piece of April 1860 (known).
- **The press abroad.** H. G. Bronn's German translation of 1860 and his correspondence with Darwin (known).
- **The commercial link.** Wallace's agent Samuel Stevens and his sale notices (check).
- **Women.** Emma Darwin's diaries are on Darwin Online (known) and should be used for more than the illness. Henrietta Darwin's letters can be added (check).
- **Wallace's own records.** His notebooks are at the Natural History Museum and the Linnean Society, *Wallace's Notebooks* project (known).
- **The colonial host.** Dutch records of Ternate, such as the Resident's reports, would give the host view (check).

**(b) Unexploited convergence.** 30 June 1860, the Oxford BAAS meeting. There are dated letters from Hooker to Darwin (2 July 1860), from Huxley, and from Lyell, plus press reports in the *Athenaeum* and *Jackson's Oxford Journal* (known). The recollections written later should be tagged as such.

**(c) Additions.** 1. Add Patrick Matthew as a full eye. It turns a two-man ledger into a real priority dispute. 2. Add the Oxford 1860 convergence day.

### The Glow in the Shed: The Curies (1895–1906)

**(a) Depth gaps.**
- **Workers.** The St Joachimsthal pitchblende residues came through Eduard Suess and the Austrian government (known). The Austrian mining-ministry correspondence could be mined (check).
- **Women beyond Marie.** Harriet Brooks at McGill, who found radioactive recoil and worked on emanation in 1901–03 (known). Marie's letters to her sister Bronisława and her father, in Polish editions (known).
- **German rivals.** Julius Elster and Hans Geitel at Wolfenbüttel (known). Friedrich Giesel at Buchler, Braunschweig, who made radium preparations from 1899 (known).
- **Vienna.** Stefan Meyer and Egon von Schweidler (known).
- **The press abroad.** The Vienna *Die Presse* X-ray scoop of 5 January 1896 (known).
- **Danger, in a US record.** Thomas Edison's X-ray work and Clarence Dally's injuries (known).
- **Non-European.** Hantaro Nagaoka's Saturnian atom of 1903–04 (known).
- **Instrument notebooks.** William Crookes's laboratory notebooks, Science Museum (check). The spectroscopist Eugène Demarçay (known as person).

**(b) Unexploited convergences.** 5–23 January 1896: the Vienna, London and Paris press, Poincaré at the Académie on 20 January, and Röntgen's Würzburg lecture on 23 January. Also 10 December 1903, the Nobel ceremony that the Curies did not attend.

**(c) Additions.** 1. Add Harriet Brooks as a McGill eye. 2. Add Giesel as the industrial-commercial radium rival.

### The Christmas Walk: Splitting the Nucleus (1934–1939)

**(a) Depth gaps.**
- **The German side after Hahn.** Paul Harteck and Wilhelm Groth's letter to the Reich War Office of 24 April 1939 (known). The Göttingen Uranverein meeting of 29 April 1939, with Georg Joos and Wilhelm Hanle (known).
- **The editor.** Paul Rosbaud of *Die Naturwissenschaften* rushed the barium paper (known).
- **The escape network.** Dirk Coster's letters on Meitner's escape (known).
- **Meitner's correspondents.** Elisabeth Schiemann and Max von Laue (known). Meitner's papers are at Churchill Archives, MTNR (known).
- **Hahn's record.** His pocket diaries in the Archiv der Max-Planck-Gesellschaft (known).
- **Paris.** Hans von Halban and Lew Kowarski, co-authors with Joliot in *Nature*, 18 March and 22 April 1939 (known).
- **US replication, January 1939.** John Dunning's Columbia notebook, 25 January (known). Merle Tuve and Richard Roberts at the Carnegie Institution's Department of Terrestrial Magnetism, 28 January (known). Luis Alvarez at Berkeley (known; memoir is later).
- **Soviet.** Georgy Flerov and Konstantin Petrzhak on spontaneous fission, 1940 (known).
- **Japan.** Yoshio Nishina's Riken replication of 1939 (check).

**(b) Unexploited convergences.** 26–28 January 1939: Washington, Columbia, DTM, Johns Hopkins and Berkeley in one 72-hour window, with the press. Late April 1939: Harteck's letter, the Göttingen meeting, and the Joliot group's neutron-number paper in *Nature*, side by side.

**(c) Additions.** 1. Add Harteck plus the Uranverein as "the other side", which the chapter lacks. 2. Add Rosbaud as the hinge between Berlin and print.

### Two Chains, Many Hands: The Double Helix (1951–1953)

**(a) Depth gaps.**
- **Chargaff.** His base ratios of 1950, his meeting with Watson and Crick in 1952, and his papers at the American Philosophical Society (known).
- **Jerry Donohue,** who in late February 1953 corrected the tautomer forms. That is the hinge of base pairing (known).
- **Lawrence Bragg's correspondence,** including the Solvay announcement of 8 April 1953 (known).
- **Earlier helical work.** Sven Furberg's 1949 Birkbeck thesis (known). William Astbury and Elwyn Beighton's 1951 Leeds B-form photograph (check).
- **Franklin's private voice.** Her letters to Adrienne Weill and her family, in Anne Sayre (1975) and the Maddox papers (known).
- **Watson's letters home** to his parents and his sister Elizabeth, Cold Spring Harbor archive (known).
- **Wilkins's letter of 18 March 1953** ("old rogues") (known).
- **Pauling's side.** Robert Corey (known). Pauling's research notebooks, Oregon State, online (known).
- **Technicians and data handlers** remain absent (check King's MRC staff files).

**(b) Unexploited convergence.** 14–19 March 1953: Peter Pauling to his father, Franklin's typescript of 17 March, Wilkins on 18 March, Crick to his son Michael on 19 March. Pauling's notebooks on his April Cambridge visit could join this window.

**(c) Additions.** 1. Add Chargaff and Donohue as eyes. Together they show the pairing rule's two outside sources. 2. Add Pauling's Oregon State notebooks for the American race.

## 4. Threads

- **telescope-1609 → edo-dissection.** Saris's 1613 telescope to Ieyasu begins the instrument trade that made rangaku possible.
- **edo-dissection ↔ darwin-wallace.** Thunberg was Linnaeus's student. The 1 July 1858 reading happened in the Linnean Society, which owns Linnaeus's collections.
- **transits-of-venus → v2:black-ships (or a Meiji chapter).** In 1874 Mexico sent Francisco Díaz Covarrubias to Yokohama to observe the next transit, and Japanese observers took part (known).
- **transits-of-venus → v7 Getting There.** The chronometer and lunar-distance trials: William Harrison's 1761–62 Jamaica voyage with H4, and Maskelyne's longitude work on St Helena.
- **telescope-1609 → v5 The Studio.** Adam Elsheimer's *Flight into Egypt* (1609) and Lodovico Cigoli's cratered Moon in Santa Maria Maggiore (1612).
- **edo-dissection → v5 The Studio.** Odano Naotake and the Akita ranga school.
- **darwin-wallace → v6 The Room.** The Linnean Society meeting of 1 July 1858 and Oxford on 30 June 1860 as "rooms".
- **fission → v6 The Room.** The Washington conference of 26 January 1939, and Bohr–Heisenberg, Copenhagen, September 1941.

## 5. Beyond this volume

- **The Ward (medicine and epidemics).** Inoculation 1721; ether 1846; cholera 1854; influenza 1918; the polio field trial and the Francis report of 12 April 1955.
- **The Sealed Envelope (priority claims as a format).** Hooke's *ut tensio* anagram; the Newton–Leibniz *Commercium epistolicum*; Bell and Gray's patent filings of 14 February 1876 (US government records); plis cachetés at the Académie; Banting and Paulescu.
- **The Other Observatory (non-Western science).** Taqi al-Din's Istanbul observatory, 1577–80; the Beijing calendar trials, 1629–69 (Xu Guangqi's memorials, Schall, Yang Guangxian, Verbiest); Jai Singh's Jantar Mantars, 1720s–30s; Inō Tadataka's survey diaries, 1800–16; J. C. Bose's 1890s papers.
