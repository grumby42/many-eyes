== intro 4
0 "On a winter morning in St Petersburg in 1869 (17 February by the Russian calendar, 1 March in the West) a chemistry professor cancelled a trip to inspect cheese cooperatives and spent the day shuffling the elements. By evening Dmitri Mendeleev had a printed sheet on its way to chemists across Europe, with blank spaces for elements nobody had seen. Seventeen days later his colleague Nikolai Menshutkin read it to the young Russian Chemical Society while its author was absent."
1 "This chapter is a priority ledger more than a diary. Its records are papers, letters to journals, a lithographed book, a printed sheet, society minutes, assay figures and medal citations, and the form tags say so. Laid side by side, they show that the idea of ordering elements by atomic weight was in the air for seven years. Chancourtois's helix appeared in Paris in 1862 with its diagram left out. Newlands's octaves were printed in a London trade weekly in 1864–65 and laughed at in the Chemical Society in 1866. Odling and Meyer printed tables in 1864, Hinrichs a spiral in Iowa in 1867, and Meyer his atomic-volume curve in 1870."
2 "What separated Mendeleev's system was not the order but the gaps. In 1871 he described three missing elements with figures: atomic weights, densities and oxides. Then the assayers and spectroscopists found them: Lecoq de Boisbaudran's gallium in 1875 in a Pyrenean zinc ore, Nilson's scandium in 1879 in Scandinavian rare earths, and Winkler's germanium in 1886, in a Saxon silver ore whose analysis came up seven per cent short. Each finder had his own relationship to the forecast. Lecoq resented being corrected on his density by a man who had never seen the metal, and found Mendeleev right. Winkler wrote to St Petersburg within three weeks."
3 "The positions are set out with their evidence and no verdict. The Royal Society's medals were the nearest thing the period had to rulings: Mendeleev and Meyer jointly in 1882, Newlands in 1887. Mendeleev's own famous story, the table seen in a dream, survives only as a colleague's later anecdote and is shown as legend beside the crossed-out drafts of the same day. The ores themselves were raised by miners at Pierrefitte and at the Himmelsfürst mine near Freiberg. No surviving firsthand account by them was found."
